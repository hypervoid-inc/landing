# Editorial Strategy

## Goal

Show what Construct can do, where each capability fits, and what its current boundaries are. Comparison pages remain useful for purchase decisions, but they should not define the publication mix.

## Publishing Mix

- 50% feature and capability stories
- 25% practical workflow guides
- 15% category education
- 10% direct comparisons

For each new comparison, publish several first-party pieces that explain a Construct capability or a real operating pattern.

### Exception: October 2026 to February 2027

The launch calendar (59 posts, Oct 1, 2026 to Feb 24, 2027) runs 28 comparisons, 47%. OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse for Small Business, Google's Gemini Spark, xAI's Grok Bot and Claude Cowork's cloud version all launched in the weeks before it, and buyers are searching for alternatives and head-to-heads that do not exist yet. The rules that keep it inside this strategy:

- Never three comparisons in a row on the calendar.
- Every comparison has a dated Methodology with the "We build Construct" disclosure, and every competitor fact is sourced.
- Review at the Nov 2, 2026 audit: if the comparison and list posts are not being cited, swap December and January comparisons for workflow guides and research before scheduling them.

From March 2027 the mix above applies again.

## Content Pillars

1. **Build with Construct:** workspace apps, internal tools, custom MCP, and reusable workflows.
2. **Execute real work:** browser, terminal, files, native email, connected apps, and scheduled jobs.
3. **Control and trust:** inspectable memory, permissions, Activity summaries, supervision, and failure boundaries.
4. **Operate across time:** recurring work, durable artifacts, team workspaces, and channels.
5. **Choose Construct:** comparisons and alternatives pages that make the case for Construct with sourced facts, and a clear path for readers already using another agent.

## Conversion Standard

Every post sells Construct. The model is `app/content/blog/construct-vs-openai-dots.mdx`.

- Construct leads: the first sentence says what Construct gives the reader, with its price or a number; tables list Construct first; rankings put Construct first.
- Competitor coverage is short, factual, and sourced. No praise, vendor marketing quotes, or testimonials for competitors. Gaps are stated as "not documented in the sources we reviewed", never guessed.
- No "where X wins" sections. At most three bullets of "When might X still fit?", late in the post.
- The verdict says "Choose Construct" and gives the reasons.
- Readers already using another agent get an "Add Construct in 15 minutes" section with numbered steps.
- Three specific calls to action: top, after pricing or the reasons, and after the verdict.
- Construct's limits appear only where a buyer would otherwise be misled, once, with the workaround.
- Every claim stays true: no invented numbers, customers, or testimonials.

## Article Standard

Every feature article should include:

- A concrete job or repeated process
- What Construct does from request to result
- The durable output or state left behind
- Supervision, permissions, or diagnostics
- Current limitations that would otherwise mislead, with the workaround
- Canonical links to related capability pages

Avoid generic AI trend posts, unsupported superlatives, and feature lists without an operating example.
