## What is an AI employee, and is AI staff the same thing?

An AI employee is AI software you assign outcomes to, not just questions. It plans a multi-step job, uses tools such as a browser, email, and connected apps to finish it, and keeps the context for the next job. AI staff, AI workers, and AI teammates are the same idea under different labels: Sintra and Marblism sell named "AI employees," Relevance AI uses "AI Workforce," and Lindy's documentation and site use both "AI employee" and "AI teammate."

Underneath the labels, three questions separate these products more than any name does:

1. **Fixed role or any task?** A roster gives you a social media manager or a receptionist with a narrow job. A general-purpose AI employee takes whatever you assign and decides which tools to use.
2. **Does it have its own computer?** An agent with a browser, files, and a terminal can work in tools that have no integration. A chat-first product is limited to what its integrations expose.
3. **How is the work metered?** Seats, credits, hours of work, and plan limits behave very differently when a busy month runs over.

Construct answers all three the way a small team needs: any task, its own workspace, and plan pricing with no seats. Our explainer on [what an AI employee is](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee/) covers the definition in more depth.

## The 10 best AI employee platforms, ranked

### 1. Construct

**Best for:** founders and small teams that want recurring work, its files, and its schedule in one place.

Construct runs your agents in a persistent cloud workspace with files, a live browser, a sandbox terminal, and its own email inbox. Give it an outcome, such as "every Monday, check these three competitors' pricing pages and save a dated comparison," and it works out the browser, app, and file steps. Everything it produces lands in workspace files as the run goes, so your team can open the result next week without scrolling back through a chat.

- **Apps:** a live integration catalog with services such as Gmail, Notion, Linear, and HubSpot, plus custom MCP tools when a catalog entry does not exist.
- **Recurring work:** save a job that works as a workflow and put it on the Calendar. Scheduled runs happen on Construct's servers, with your laptop closed.
- **Memory:** inspect what the agent has learned, correct a fact, or forget it, so one fix carries into every later job.
- **Internal apps:** when several people review the same output, Construct builds a private workspace app, such as a review queue or lead tracker.
- **Team use:** team workspaces with Owner, Admin, and Member roles and no member limit. Usage draws on the owner's plan, so there is no per-seat bill.
- **Channels:** assign work from the web desktop, @mention it in Slack, message it on Telegram or Discord, or email its own address.
- **Models:** bundled models on every plan, and on Pro your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI.

### 2. CellCog

**Best for:** hiring one AI employee into a defined role that works in scheduled shifts.

CellCog lets you hire a general-purpose AI employee into a role. Each one gets an email inbox, a task board, persistent memory, and a cloud computer, and with your approval it can work in your own logged-in Chrome. It works scheduled shifts, files a report after each one, and lists more than 1,500 tool integrations.

**Price:** credit plans start at $8 a month for 800 credits and rise to $500 a month for 50,000. CellCog's own plan labels call the $160 tier a part-time employee working "a few shifts a month" and the $500 tier a full-time employee working weekday shifts. New accounts start with 200 free credits.

**Watch for:** CellCog's pricing FAQ says all payments are final and non-refundable, and plan credits expire. Its pages do not name the underlying model providers or offer bring-your-own keys.

### 3. Manus

**Best for:** research, analysis, and build tasks you hand over one at a time.

Each Manus task runs in a sandbox virtual machine with a browser, persistent files, and the ability to install software. Manus 2.0, launched September 28, 2026, adds a desktop app, an always-on Cloud Computer, and control of your own machine. Automations run on a schedule or on app events, and you can reach it by email, Slack, or Telegram.

**Price:** Pro starts at $20 a month for 4,000 credits plus 300 refresh credits a day, with 20 scheduled tasks. Team is $20 per seat a month with a shared credit pool. The always-on Cloud Computer is an add-on from $30 a month.

**Watch for:** credit use varies with task complexity, monthly credits do not roll over, and you cannot start a task with zero credits. If the subscription lapses, the Cloud Computer shuts down and its files are deleted. Manus returned to [independent operation](https://manus.im/blog/manus-resumes-independent-operations) on September 1, 2026 after Meta's acquisition was unwound.

### 4. Lindy

**Best for:** teams whose work already happens in Slack.

Lindy Teammate is an AI coworker in Slack: each person gets a private assistant in direct messages and the team shares one in channels. It connects to an integration catalog and MCP servers, can drive a browser, runs Routines on a schedule or on events, keeps memory as versioned files, and reaches you over iMessage and SMS. Write actions wait for your approval.

**Price:** billed per user. Plus is $29.99 a month with 3,000 credits, Pro is $99.99 with 15,000, and Max is $199.99 with 35,000. Lindy's docs put a credit at about one cent and pool credits across the workspace.

**Watch for:** anyone who uses Lindy takes a paid seat, including someone who only mentions it in Slack. When the credit pool runs out, work pauses until you top up. Lindy's pricing page lists computer use as included everywhere, while its docs show it from Pro upward.

### 5. Grok Bot

**Best for:** people who already pay for Cursor or an eligible SuperGrok plan.

Grok Bot gives you named Bots that share one persistent cloud computer with a browser, files, and a terminal. They sign into your tools, run routines on a schedule or on events, can learn a routine by watching you, and ask for approval before actions you have not allowed. You work with them through desktop and mobile apps.

**Price:** there is no standalone plan. Access is included with Cursor Pro at $20 a month, Pro+, Ultra, and Teams, or with an eligible SuperGrok or X Premium+ subscription linked to a Cursor account. Usage resets weekly and is described in relative tiers rather than published numbers.

**Watch for:** it is labeled beta. Because Bots share one computer, its documentation says not to treat separate Bots as a security boundary. Each Bot can hold up to 50 routines. Our [Grok Bot comparison](/blog/grokbot-alternative/) goes deeper on how it differs from Construct.

### 6. Sintra

**Best for:** a solo owner who wants marketing, social, and admin help without designing anything.

Sintra sells 12 named AI employees, including Soshie for social media, Penn for copywriting, Seomi for SEO, Cassie for customer support, and Vizzy as an executive assistant. They share a knowledge base called Brain AI, run recurring tasks, and can use a built-in browser. Sintra's own FAQ says every AI worker runs as a chatbot.

**Price:** one plan, Sintra X, includes all 12. It lists at $97 a month, and when we checked it was on sale at $48.50 for a one-month term, with lower monthly equivalents for 3- and 12-month terms billed upfront. Every term includes 250 credits a month.

**Watch for:** Sintra's help center says the AI employees stop working when credits run out, and credit top-ups are non-refundable. Its own pages disagree with each other on standard prices and on how many integrations it has.

### 7. Marblism

**Best for:** a local business that wants calls answered along with inbox and social help.

Marblism's roster has seven AI employees: Eva (executive assistant), Sonny (social media), Stan (lead generation), Penny (SEO blog posts), Rachel (phone receptionist), Linda (legal documents), and Walter (websites). Rachel answers calls, transfers them, and texts you summaries, with numbers available in five countries.

**Price:** the base plan is $44 a month, or $24 a month billed yearly, for all seven employees and 50 "hours of work." Each task costs a fixed amount of that time: sorting an email is 20 seconds, a phone call 10 minutes, and a blog post an hour. Larger plans go up to 10,000 hours.

**Watch for:** Marblism documents that Eva drafts emails but does not send them. It has no public API and says one is not on its roadmap. When hours run out, scheduled tasks are cancelled and Rachel's line pauses.

### 8. Relevance AI

**Best for:** sales and support teams that want to design their own agents and multi-agent teams.

You build agents and multi-agent Workforces in Relevance AI's low-code builder, or ask its Invent agent to draft one, and connect them to its app catalog. Agents can run on a schedule, call Python tools, make outbound phone calls on higher tiers, and use the model you pick, including through your own keys.

**Price:** the public pricing page shows only an Enterprise plan with a "Talk to sales" button. Relevance AI's documentation still lists Pro at $29 a month ($19 billed annually) with 2,500 actions and $20 of model and tool credits, and Team at $349 a month. Confirm with the vendor before you plan around either.

**Watch for:** there is no dedicated agent computer, and browser automation runs through a third-party tool, Airtop, on your own account. Failed tool runs still count as actions. SSO and audit logs are Enterprise features.

### 9. OpenClaw

**Best for:** technical owners who want full control and are comfortable running a server.

OpenClaw is a free, MIT-licensed personal AI agent stewarded by a non-profit foundation. You run its gateway on your own computer or a small server, connect any model with your own keys, and chat with it from WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, iMessage, and other channels. It can browse, run shell commands, edit files, keep memory, and run cron jobs.

**Price:** the software costs nothing, and there is no paid tier. You pay for hosting, where its docs suggest a small always-on server, and for model usage.

**Watch for:** installing, updating, securing, and keeping it online is your job. Its security documentation assumes one trusted operator per gateway, says third-party skills should be treated as untrusted code, and warns that container setups expose the gateway by default and need authentication. Our comparison of [building your own AI agent](/blog/construct-vs-diy/) covers that tradeoff.

### 10. Artisan

**Best for:** outbound sales teams that want an AI BDR to prospect and write outreach.

Artisan's Ava finds and enriches leads from a database of more than 250 million contacts, writes and sends email sequences from your own mailboxes, handles replies, and books meetings. It is a sales employee only.

**Price:** Artisan's default pricing tab is sales-led. A separate Startups track lists an Intern plan from $280 a month ($250 billed annually) for about 500 leads contacted, plus a free plan without campaigns. Mailboxes and phone numbers are billed separately.

**Watch for:** Artisan says Ava cannot legally make calls, so its dialer is for your human reps. Each primary mailbox is capped at 100 sends a day, and new enrollments pause when credits run out.

## How to choose an AI employee platform

Choose by the first job you will hand over, where requests arrive, and what each platform does in a busy month. In order:

1. **Start from the job, not the category.** Write down the one job you would hand over first. If it crosses apps or changes from week to week, you need a general-purpose AI employee such as Construct; a roster covers only its named roles.
2. **Check where the work arrives.** Construct takes requests from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord, and its own email inbox, so it meets your team where it already talks.
3. **Price the busy month.** Assume twice the work you expect and check what each vendor does at the limit.
4. **Test recurring work, not a demo.** Run the same job three weeks in a row and check whether later runs start from the earlier files and corrections. Construct keeps both in the workspace.
5. **Know what you can inspect and correct.** Ask where memory lives, whether you can edit it, and what record a run leaves behind. [AI agent memory you can control](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) explains why that matters.
6. **Decide who operates it.** Self-hosting saves the subscription and spends your time instead.
