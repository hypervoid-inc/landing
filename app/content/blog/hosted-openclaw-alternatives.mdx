**Construct gives you an always-on AI employee from $9 a month, at a fixed monthly price with model usage inside published plan limits and no server, gateway, or framework for you to run.** OpenClaw itself has no hosted version, so "hosted OpenClaw" always means a third party runs it for you, from $5.99 a month paid upfront at Hostinger to $29 a month at MyClaw, plus your model usage, and the agent's security settings are still yours to configure.

For founders and small teams who want the work done rather than an agent to operate, Construct is the better choice. You get a fixed monthly price with published plan limits instead of a hosting fee plus a separate token bill, recurring jobs that run on a schedule you set rather than a heartbeat that spends tokens every 30 minutes, and finished files in a workspace your whole team can open. If you specifically need OpenClaw's channels or skills, a managed host is the way to run it, and this guide covers the main ones.

The OpenClaw Foundation is explicit that it sells no hosting. Its homepage says "No subscription. No hosted tier. No token," and its September 29 announcement of OpenClaw Enterprise describes software "built to run on your own infrastructure" ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/), [OpenClaw Enterprise](https://openclaw.ai/blog/openclaw-enterprise)). We checked every product on October 1, 2026.

## Why choose Construct over a hosted OpenClaw?

- **A fixed monthly price that covers the model.** Construct Lite is $9 a month for 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Plans include model usage within published limits, with no per-message billing.
- **Nothing to host, patch, or harden.** There is no gateway on the internet, no SSH, and no sandbox or exec-approval settings to get right. Shell commands run in a sandbox, and stored model keys and bot credentials are encrypted.
- **Background work only when you schedule it.** Jobs on the Calendar run server-side at the times you set, with your laptop closed: 3 on Lite, 10 on Starter, 50 on Pro.
- **Files your team can open.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to the workspace as they are produced, so a run that stops at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29. Team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles and no member limit.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Inspect what the agent has learned, correct a fact, or tell it to forget one.
- **Your own keys when you want them.** Pro supports keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI.

## Why look for OpenClaw without self-hosting?

OpenClaw is an MIT-licensed agent that answers on WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, iMessage, Microsoft Teams, and other channels, keeps persistent memory, runs cron jobs, drives a browser, and runs shell commands ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/)). The reasons people look for a hosted version are about operating it.

**It has to stay on.** OpenClaw's FAQ says: "Want 24/7 reliability? Use a VPS." On a laptop, "sleep/network drops disconnect it, OS updates/reboots interrupt it, must stay awake." A VPS is "often headless," gives "remote file access only," and needs "SSH needed for updates" ([OpenClaw FAQ: providers, hardware, and hosting](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq-first-run/providers-and-hosting)).

**Security is your configuration.** The "Why OpenClaw" page states that "Sandboxing and exec approvals are off by default" and "Hardening is deliberate configuration" ([Why OpenClaw](https://docs.openclaw.ai/start/why-openclaw)). Its security FAQ has an entry titled "I saw reports about exposed OpenClaw instances. What should I check?" ([OpenClaw security FAQ](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq/security-and-access-control)).

**Mistakes happen on real accounts.** In February 2026, Meta AI security researcher Summer Yue described an OpenClaw agent that started deleting her real inbox and ignored her stop commands, and blamed context compaction for the agent losing her final instruction ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/a-meta-ai-security-researcher-said-an-openclaw-agent-ran-amok-on-her-inbox/)). A host does not change that; "always on" also means "always able to act."

**Token bills are separate and can surprise you.** OpenClaw's heartbeat runs a check-in every 30 minutes by default, and the docs warn that "Heartbeats run full agent turns. Shorter intervals burn more tokens." Isolating heartbeat sessions cuts a run from "~100K tokens down to ~2-5K" ([OpenClaw heartbeat docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat)).

As illustrative arithmetic from the docs, not a measured bill: 48 heartbeats a day at about 100,000 tokens each is roughly 4.8 million input tokens a day before the agent does any work you asked for. With isolated sessions at 2,000 to 5,000 tokens, the same schedule is about 96,000 to 240,000. Whoever hosts your OpenClaw, check this setting first.

## What does a hosted claw really cost per month?

This is an illustrative comparison using list prices, not a measured test. A solo founder wants an agent that sends a morning briefing, checks three competitor pricing pages every Monday, and drafts replies to inbound email.

## A managed AI employee instead of a managed claw

### 1. Construct

**Best for:** founders and small teams whose recurring work crosses several apps and should leave files behind, without an agent framework to maintain.

Construct is a hosted AI employee, not an OpenClaw host. Each agent works from a persistent cloud workspace with files, a live browser, a sandbox terminal, a native agent email address, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches apps such as Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable through a live integration catalog, and you can add custom MCP tools for anything the catalog does not cover. You assign work from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord slash commands, or by emailing the agent's own inbox; only mail from your own address starts a run.

Nothing runs on your laptop and there is no gateway to secure. Content the agent reads from web pages, email, and connected apps reaches the model marked as external data whose instructions it should not follow. That reduces prompt-injection risk rather than removing it, so check early runs and keep irreversible steps manual.

## Managed OpenClaw hosts

These run the real OpenClaw for you. You still configure the agent, its channels, and what it can touch; the host handles the machine, updates, and uptime.

### 2. Hostinger 1-Click OpenClaw

**Best for:** the lowest entry price for a managed OpenClaw.

Hostinger's managed OpenClaw plan lists Telegram pairing, pre-configured AI credits, the option to use your ChatGPT, web search, and agentic email ([Hostinger OpenClaw](https://www.hostinger.com/openclaw)). OpenClaw's Hostinger guide adds that you can buy AI credits at checkout or bring a key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, or xAI, and that a self-managed VPS option is also sold ([OpenClaw docs: Hostinger](https://docs.openclaw.ai/install/hostinger)).

**Price:** $5.99 a month, discounted from $21.99, with "Renews at $11.99/mo for 2 years" and "All plans are paid upfront," so the first payment is larger than $5.99. AI credits are topped up separately. There is a 30-day money-back guarantee. The heartbeat and sandbox settings above are still yours to set.

### 3. MyClaw

**Best for:** OpenClaw or Hermes Agent on a private instance with backups.

MyClaw hosts OpenClaw and Hermes Agent, reachable from email, Telegram, WhatsApp, or a browser ([MyClaw](https://myclaw.ai/)). Every plan includes uptime, auto-updates, encryption, and daily backups ([MyClaw pricing](https://myclaw.ai/pricing)). It is run by Cubo World Inc., according to its site footer, and is not part of the OpenClaw project.

**Price:** Lite is $29 a month, or $199 a year (about $16.60 a month), for 2 vCPU, 4 GB of RAM, and 40 GB of storage. Pro is $59 monthly or $399 a year with $100 of AI credits; Max is $119 monthly or $799 a year with $150. The footnote says "AI token usage isn't included in the subscription," so plan for a model bill on Lite.

### 4. Genspark Claw

**Best for:** a messaging-first assistant with schedule templates.

Genspark Claw runs on a cloud computer or locally through a desktop app, answers in WhatsApp, Slack, Teams, Telegram, LINE, Discord, Signal, Google Chat, Feishu, and more, has its own email address, and has a Schedules tab with templates ([Genspark Claw help center](https://www.genspark.ai/helpcenter/genspark-claw)). You connect services by logging in through a remote desktop. Sliq says Claw launched on March 12, 2026 and wraps the OpenClaw framework in a managed service ([Sliq](https://getsliq.com/blog/genspark-claw-explained)); Genspark's help center does not mention OpenClaw.

**Price:** the cloud computer comes in Lite, Standard, and Powerful tiers (2 vCPU and 4 GB of RAM up to 4 vCPU and 16 GB), with prices on a Claw page that returned HTTP 403 to us ([Genspark membership plans](https://www.genspark.ai/helpcenter/membership-plans)). Sliq lists Standard at $39.99 a month and Powerful at $79.99. AI work also uses Genspark credits, and Genspark says scheduled tasks and Heartbeat "consume credits in the background, even when you're not actively using Claw." ## Running it yourself, with less setup

### 6. One-click deploy templates

**Best for:** technical owners who want their own instance without a hand-built server.

OpenClaw's Linux server guide lists hosting walkthroughs for Azure, Daytona, DigitalOcean, exe.dev, Fly.io, GCP, Hetzner, Hostinger, Northflank, Oracle Cloud, Railway, Render, Raspberry Pi, and Upstash Box, with Railway and Northflank marked "One-click, browser setup" ([OpenClaw docs: Linux server](https://docs.openclaw.ai/vps)). You pay the provider plus your model bill. A template gets you a running gateway, not a managed one: the docs still tell you to back up state, keep the gateway behind an SSH tunnel or Tailscale, and harden admin access.

### 7. Self-hosted OpenClaw

**Best for:** owners who want to run the agent, its models, and its data entirely themselves.

The software is free. The minimum server is "1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, ~500 MB disk," with 2 GB or more recommended for browser automation and several channels ([OpenClaw FAQ](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq-first-run/providers-and-hosting)). Install is `curl -fsSL https://openclaw.ai/install.sh | bash` or `npm i -g openclaw` ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/)), with any model provider or a local model. You run the box, the updates, the security audit, and the token budget. Our guide to [building your own AI agent](/blog/construct-vs-diy/) covers that trade.

## Already running OpenClaw? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to shut your claw down. Move the recurring job that costs you the most tokens or the most babysitting, and keep OpenClaw for the channels only it covers.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test longer runs and your own model keys.
2. **Copy the instructions** your agent follows for that job into a Construct chat, and move its reference files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, or add a custom MCP tool.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs on schedule whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import. OpenClaw skills, memory files, and channel pairings do not transfer, so describe what a skill did in your instruction and Construct saves the rules as memory you can read.

## When might a hosted claw still fit?

- You need the agent on WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage, or Microsoft Teams.
- You want to run a local model or modify the agent's source.
- You need a process that runs continuously; each Construct sandbox command has a five-minute runtime cap on every plan.

For recurring business work with shared files, correctable memory, and a fixed monthly price, Construct is the better fit.
