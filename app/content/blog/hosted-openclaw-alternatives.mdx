**Construct gives you what most people want from hosted OpenClaw, an always-on AI employee, for $9 a month, with model usage included within published plan limits and no server, gateway, or framework for you to run.** OpenClaw itself has no hosted version, so "hosted OpenClaw" always means a third party runs it for you, from $5.99 a month paid upfront at Hostinger to $29 a month at MyClaw, plus your model usage, and the agent's security settings are still yours to configure.

For founders and small teams who want the work done rather than an agent to operate, Construct is the better choice. You get a fixed monthly price with published plan limits instead of a hosting fee plus a separate token bill, recurring jobs that run on a schedule you set rather than a heartbeat that spends tokens every 30 minutes, and finished files in a workspace your whole team can open. If you specifically need OpenClaw's channels or skills, a managed host is the way to run it, and this guide covers the main ones.

## Why choose Construct over a hosted OpenClaw?

- **A fixed monthly price that covers the model.** Construct Lite is $9 a month for 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Plans include model usage within published limits, with no per-message billing.
- **Nothing to host, patch, or harden.** There is no gateway on the internet, no SSH, and no sandbox or exec-approval settings to get right. Shell commands run in a sandbox, and stored model keys and bot credentials are encrypted.
- **Background work only when you schedule it.** Jobs on the Calendar run server-side at the times you set, with your laptop closed: 3 on Lite, 10 on Starter, 50 on Pro.
- **Files your team can open.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to the workspace as they are produced, so a run that stops at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29. Team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles and no member limit.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Inspect what the agent has learned, correct a fact, or tell it to forget one.
- **Your own keys when you want them.** Pro supports keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI.

## Is there an official hosted OpenClaw?

No. The OpenClaw Foundation sells no hosting: its homepage says "No subscription. No hosted tier. No token," and adds that there is no enterprise edition and no paid version ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/)). OpenClaw Enterprise, announced on September 29, 2026, "is built to run on your own infrastructure and will always be free for any organization to use" ([OpenClaw Enterprise](https://openclaw.ai/blog/openclaw-enterprise)). Every hosted OpenClaw is a third-party service. We checked every product below on October 11, 2026.

## Is OpenClaw free?

The software is free; running it is not. OpenClaw is MIT licensed, and you pay for the machine it runs on and for the model it calls, either through your own API keys or a host's AI credits. A managed host adds its own fee on top. The model bill is the part people underestimate, because of how often the agent wakes up.

## Why look for OpenClaw without self-hosting?

OpenClaw is an agent that answers on channels including WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, iMessage, Microsoft Teams, and Google Chat, keeps memory, runs cron jobs, drives a browser, and runs commands ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/)). The reasons people look for a hosted version are about operating it.

**It has to stay on.** OpenClaw's FAQ says: "Want 24/7 reliability? Use a VPS." On a laptop, "sleep/network drops disconnect it, OS updates/reboots interrupt it, must stay awake." A VPS is "often headless," gives "remote file access only," and needs "SSH needed for updates" ([OpenClaw FAQ: providers, hardware, and hosting](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq-first-run/providers-and-hosting)).

**Security is your configuration.** The "Why OpenClaw" page states that "Sandboxing and exec approvals are off by default" and "Hardening is deliberate configuration" ([Why OpenClaw](https://docs.openclaw.ai/start/why-openclaw)). Its security FAQ has an entry titled "I saw reports about exposed OpenClaw instances. What should I check?", and the first things it lists are public binds without auth, open DMs or groups with tools, and exposed browser control ([OpenClaw security FAQ](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq/security-and-access-control)).

**Mistakes happen on real accounts.** In February 2026, Meta AI security researcher Summer Yue described an OpenClaw agent that started deleting her real inbox and ignored her stop commands, and she believes the size of the inbox triggered context compaction ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/a-meta-ai-security-researcher-said-an-openclaw-agent-ran-amok-on-her-inbox/)). A host does not change that; "always on" also means "always able to act."

**Token bills are separate and can surprise you.** OpenClaw's heartbeat runs a check-in every 30 minutes by default, or every hour when Anthropic OAuth or token auth is configured, and the docs warn that "Heartbeats run full agent turns. Shorter intervals burn more tokens." Isolating heartbeat sessions cuts a run from "~100K tokens down to ~2-5K" ([OpenClaw heartbeat docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat)).

As illustrative arithmetic from the docs, not a measured bill: 48 heartbeats a day at about 100,000 tokens each is roughly 4.8 million input tokens a day before the agent does any work you asked for. With isolated sessions at 2,000 to 5,000 tokens, the same schedule is about 96,000 to 240,000. Whoever hosts your OpenClaw, check this setting first.

## How much does hosted OpenClaw cost per month?

Hosted OpenClaw starts at $5.99 a month paid upfront at Hostinger, or $29 billed monthly at MyClaw, and both leave the model bill on top; Construct is $9 with model usage included. This is an illustrative comparison using list prices, not a measured test. A solo founder wants an agent that sends a morning briefing, checks three competitor pricing pages every Monday, and drafts replies to inbound email.

## A managed AI employee instead of a managed claw

### 1. Construct

**Best for:** founders and small teams whose recurring work crosses several apps and should leave files behind, without an agent framework to maintain.

Construct is a hosted AI employee, not an OpenClaw host. Each agent works from a persistent cloud workspace with files, a live browser, a sandbox terminal, a native agent email address, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches apps such as Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable through a live integration catalog, and you can add custom MCP tools for anything the catalog does not cover. You assign work from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord slash commands, or by emailing the agent's own inbox; only mail from your own address starts a run ([Construct across channels](/use-cases/channels/)).

Nothing runs on your laptop and there is no gateway to secure. Content the agent reads from web pages, email, and connected apps reaches the model marked as external data whose instructions it should not follow. That reduces prompt-injection risk rather than removing it, so check early runs and keep irreversible steps manual.

## What is the best managed OpenClaw hosting?

For the lowest price, Hostinger; for a private instance with backups and Hermes support, MyClaw. These run the real OpenClaw for you. You still configure the agent, its channels, and what it can touch; the host handles the machine, updates, and uptime.

### 2. Hostinger 1-Click OpenClaw

**Best for:** the lowest entry price for a managed OpenClaw.

Hostinger's managed OpenClaw plan lists Telegram pairing, pre-configured AI credits, the option to use your ChatGPT, web search, and pre-configured agentic email, and its FAQ names WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, and Discord ([Hostinger OpenClaw](https://www.hostinger.com/openclaw)). OpenClaw's Hostinger guide adds that you can buy AI credits at checkout or bring a key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, or xAI, and that a self-managed VPS option is also sold ([OpenClaw docs: Hostinger](https://docs.openclaw.ai/install/hostinger)).

**Price:** $5.99 a month, discounted from $21.99, with "Renews at $11.99/mo for 2 years" and "All plans are paid upfront," so the first payment is larger than $5.99. How many AI credits come included is not documented on the page, and top-ups are bought from the dashboard. There is a 30-day money-back guarantee. The heartbeat and sandbox settings above are still yours to set.

### 3. MyClaw

**Best for:** OpenClaw or Hermes Agent on a private instance with backups.

MyClaw hosts OpenClaw and Hermes Agent, reachable from email, Telegram, WhatsApp, or a browser, with always-on hosting, auto-updates, encryption, and daily backups ([MyClaw](https://myclaw.ai/)). It is run by Cubo World Inc., according to its site footer, and is not part of the OpenClaw project.

**Price:** Lite is $29 a month, or $199 a year (about $16.60 a month), for 2 vCPU, 4 GB of RAM, and 40 GB of storage. Pro is $59 monthly or $399 a year with $100 of AI credits, Max is $119 monthly or $799 a year with $150, and Ultra is $239 monthly or $1,599 a year with $200 ([MyClaw pricing](https://myclaw.ai/pricing)). The footnote says "AI token usage isn't included in the subscription," so plan for a model bill on Lite.

### 4. Cloudways Managed AI Agents

**Best for:** OpenClaw or Hermes from a large hosting company you may already use.

Cloudways, part of DigitalOcean, runs OpenClaw and Hermes on dedicated managed infrastructure from its dashboard, handling provisioning, security patching, and upkeep, with Slack, Discord, Telegram, and WhatsApp for both agents ([Cloudways](https://www.cloudways.com/en/blog/cloudways-managed-ai-agents/)).

**Price:** Cloudways says the service is generally available and that standard pricing is in effect, but the announcement lists no plan prices, and we found no published price list in the pages we reviewed. Check the dashboard before you commit, and budget for the model separately unless the plan says otherwise.

## Other managed claws and hosted Hermes

### 5. Genspark Claw

**Best for:** a messaging-first assistant with schedule templates.

Genspark Claw runs on an always-on cloud computer or locally through a desktop app that only works while your computer is on. It answers in WhatsApp, Telegram, LINE, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Signal, Google Chat, and Feishu, has its own email address, and has a Schedules tab with templates ([Genspark Claw help center](https://www.genspark.ai/helpcenter/genspark-claw)). You connect services by logging in through a remote desktop. Sliq says Claw launched on March 12, 2026 and wraps the OpenClaw framework in a managed service ([Sliq](https://getsliq.com/blog/genspark-claw-explained)); Genspark's help center does not mention OpenClaw.

**Price:** the cloud computer comes in Lite, Standard, and Powerful tiers, from 2 vCPU and 4 GB of RAM up to 4 vCPU and 16 GB, at a flat monthly fee ([Genspark membership plans](https://www.genspark.ai/helpcenter/membership-plans)). The pricing data that [genspark.ai/claw](https://web.archive.org/web/20261010065156/https://www.genspark.ai/claw) loads, checked October 11, 2026, lists Lite at $9.99 a month, Standard at $19.99, and Powerful at $39.99 ($104.99, $209.99, and $419.99 billed annually), shown as discounted prices. The Genspark Plus membership for credits is $24.99 a month. AI work also uses Genspark credits, and Genspark says scheduled tasks and Heartbeat "consume credits in the background, even when you're not actively using Claw." Cancelling recycles the cloud computer, and all data on it is permanently deleted.

### 6. Hermes Agent on Nous Portal

**Best for:** people who already use Hermes Agent and want it running around the clock.

Hermes Agent is Nous Research's MIT-licensed agent, with skills it builds from experience, persistent memory, a cron scheduler, and one gateway for Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, email, the command line, and more ([Hermes Agent on GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)). Nous Portal offers Agent Cloud Hosting: "Deploy in one click and Portal hosts your agent in the cloud, running around the clock. Server costs bill straight to your credit balance" ([Nous Portal](https://portal.nousresearch.com/)).

**Price:** Free is $0 for free models only. Plus is $20 a month with $22 of monthly credits, Super is $100 with $110, and Ultra is $200 with $220. Credits cover models, hosted tools, and server time. You can also use a ChatGPT plan for the model with no Nous subscription, in which case tools and cloud hosting are charged separately. The server cost per month is not documented in the Nous pages we reviewed.

## Running it yourself, with less setup

### 7. One-click deploy templates

**Best for:** technical owners who want their own instance without a hand-built server.

OpenClaw's Linux server guide lists hosting walkthroughs for 14 providers, including Azure, DigitalOcean, Fly.io, GCP, Hetzner, Hostinger, Oracle Cloud, Railway, Render, and Northflank, with Railway and Northflank marked "One-click, browser setup" ([OpenClaw docs: Linux server](https://docs.openclaw.ai/vps)). You pay the provider plus your model bill. A template gets you a running gateway, not a managed one: the docs still tell you to back up the state and workspace regularly, keep the gateway on loopback behind an SSH tunnel or Tailscale Serve, and decide how you will administer the box.

### 8. Self-hosted OpenClaw

**Best for:** owners who want to run the agent, its models, and its data entirely themselves.

The software is free. The minimum server is "1 vCPU, 1 GB RAM, ~500 MB disk," with 2 GB or more recommended because browser automation can be resource hungry ([OpenClaw FAQ](https://docs.openclaw.ai/help/faq-first-run/providers-and-hosting)). Install is `curl -fsSL https://openclaw.ai/install.sh | bash` or `npm i -g openclaw` ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/)), with any model provider or a local model. You run the box, the updates, the security audit, and the token budget. Our guide to [building your own AI agent](/blog/construct-vs-diy/) covers that trade.

## How to compare a hosted claw with Construct yourself

Run one week of the same three jobs on the host you are considering and on Construct Lite. We have not published results of our own; this is the checklist to follow.

1. **Use the same three jobs:** a morning briefing, a Monday competitor price check saved to a dated file, and inbound email reply drafts with nothing sent.
2. **Time the setup,** from sign-up to the first usable result, including any channel pairing, keys, or sandbox settings.
3. **Record the model spend each day,** with the heartbeat on and off, from the host's dashboard or your provider's usage page.
4. **Note every failure and retry,** and whether the agent picked up where it stopped.
5. **Check where each result was saved,** and whether a teammate can open it without your login.

Here is how the Construct side runs, as an illustrative walkthrough rather than a measured result. Save the three competitor URLs in a workspace file. Ask the agent to visit each pricing page, write a dated CSV, and compare it with last week's file. Check the first result, then save the steps as a workflow and put it on the Calendar. There is no server to provision and no heartbeat to tune before the first run. The [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) walks through each step.

## Already running OpenClaw? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to shut your claw down. Move the recurring job that costs you the most tokens or the most babysitting, and keep OpenClaw for the channels only it covers.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test longer runs and your own model keys.
2. **Copy the instructions** your agent follows for that job into a Construct chat, and move its reference files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, or add a custom MCP tool.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs on schedule whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import. OpenClaw skills, memory files, and channel pairings do not transfer, so describe what a skill did in your instruction and Construct saves the rules as memory you can read.

## When might a hosted claw still fit?

- You need the agent on WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage, or Microsoft Teams.
- You want to run a local model or modify the agent's source.
- You need a process that runs continuously; each Construct terminal command can run for up to 5 minutes, so split longer jobs into steps.

For recurring business work with shared files, correctable memory, and a fixed monthly price, Construct is the better fit.
