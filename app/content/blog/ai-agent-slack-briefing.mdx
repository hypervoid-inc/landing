## What should a morning briefing include?

A useful morning briefing covers four things: today's meetings, email that needs your reply, what changed overnight, and one decision waiting on you. If it does not save you opening four tabs, it is not worth reading.

- **Today's meetings**, with who is attending and anything you were asked to prepare.
- **Email that needs a reply from you**, oldest first, one line each.
- **What changed overnight** in the files the team watches.
- **One thing to decide today**, if anything is waiting on you.

Keep it short enough to read on a phone before the first call, which in practice means under 200 words. The agent can save a longer version to Files for anyone who wants the detail.

## Before you start

Four things shape the setup: scheduled runs report in Construct rather than posting to Slack, the agent in Slack only sees messages addressed to it, channel mentions run on the installer's account, and the agent's inbox has a daily sending limit.

- **The channel gets the briefing by email.** A scheduled run cannot post into a Slack channel by itself yet; its output lands in the run's chat in Construct with a notification. Emailing the channel's address, as in step 5, puts the briefing in Slack every morning. It appears as a posted email rather than a bot message, and Slack lets you give those posts a custom name and image.
- **Point it at sources it can read.** In Slack the agent sees messages that mention it, replies in the threads those mentions start, and direct messages. It does not read the rest of the channel, so build the briefing from calendar, email, and files, as the prompts here do.
- **Channel requests run as the installer.** A mention in a channel uses the installer's Construct account, its connected apps, and its plan's usage, so give **Full access** only to people you would trust with those apps. A direct message runs on the sender's own Construct account instead, once they have linked it.
- **Email the channel once a day.** The agent's inbox has a daily sending limit enforced on the server, and a scheduled run can occasionally be delivered twice, which is why the scheduled prompt checks for today's file before sending.

## How do you set up a Slack daily briefing?

Add Construct to Slack, get the channel's email address, run the briefing once while you watch, save the format, then schedule it.

### 1. Add Construct to Slack

In the Construct desktop, open **Settings**, then **Messaging**, and choose **Add to Slack**. Approve the install in Slack. You are now the installer, which always has full access.

Under access, the default is **Only people I allow**: everyone else is ignored. Add teammates who should be able to ask for the briefing to the **Full access** list. If you choose **Everyone in the server** instead, people not on the list get a read-only guest agent that can search the web but cannot use your connected apps, files, or email.

### 2. Get the Slack channel's email address

Yes, you can email a Slack channel on a paid plan. Pick the channel the briefing should land in, such as #daily. Open the channel, click the channel name in the header, open the **Automations** tab, choose the option to send emails to this channel, and click **Get email address** ([Slack Help Center](https://slack.com/help/articles/206819278-Send-emails-to-Slack)). Copy the address.

Slack says "by default, any member of a channel can get an email address for a channel or DM," and owners and admins can turn that off. If the option is missing, ask your Slack admin. From the same tab, the email appearance setting lets you choose the name and icon the briefing posts under.

On Slack's free plan, a personal alternative is the Slackbot forwarding address, available on all plans: in **Preferences**, **Messages & media**, choose **Get a forwarding address**, and mail sent there arrives in your own DM with Slackbot ([Slack Help Center](https://slack.com/help/articles/206819278-Send-emails-to-Slack)). That works for a briefing only you read.

### 3. Run the morning briefing prompt once

In Construct, ask for today's briefing while you watch:

<Prompt>

Prepare my morning briefing. Include: today's Google Calendar meetings with attendees and anything I was asked to prepare; Gmail threads from the last 24 hours that need a reply from me, oldest first, one line each; and any file in Files/clients/ that changed since yesterday. End with the one decision I most need to make today, or "none". Keep it under 200 words. Save it to Files/briefings/ named with today's date. Do not send or reply to any email.

</Prompt>

Read the file. If it is too long, missed a meeting, or called a newsletter urgent, tell the agent what to change and run it again. If newsletters keep slipping in, label them in Gmail first (our free [Gmail filter generator](/tools/gmail-filter-generator/) builds the filters) and tell the agent to skip that label.

### 4. Make the format stick

When you like the result, ask the agent to remember the rules so tomorrow's run does not depend on today's chat:

<Prompt>

Remember this briefing format: meetings first, then replies needed, then file changes, then one decision. Under 200 words. Treat newsletters and automated mail as not needing a reply. Never reply to or send email while preparing the briefing.

</Prompt>

You can check and correct what it stored in the Memories app later. Our [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) explains how.

### 5. Schedule it and send it to the channel

Put it on the Calendar from the same chat, with the channel address from step 2:

<Prompt>

Every weekday at 7:45am in my time zone, prepare my morning briefing using the format you remembered and save it to Files/briefings/. Then email the briefing from your own address to daily-abc123@yourteam.slack.com with the subject "Briefing" and today's date. Send nothing else. If today's briefing file already exists, do not send it again.

</Prompt>

The address in that prompt is an example; use the one Slack gave you. The agent creates a Calendar event that repeats every weekday and belongs to that agent. Open it and set **Delivery behavior** to **Run once** for a late occurrence, so a delayed morning sends one briefing rather than a backlog. Then press **Run now** to check the email arrives in the channel. Our [scheduling guide](/blog/ai-agent-scheduled-tasks/) explains each setting, and the [scheduling overview](/use-cases/scheduling/) shows what else can run on the Calendar.

### 6. Ask for it in Slack when you want it

The agent also answers in Slack directly. Mention it in a channel, or send it a direct message:

<Prompt>

@Construct brief me. Read today's file in Files/briefings/ and post the summary here. If it is missing, prepare it now.

</Prompt>

In a channel, the agent replies in the thread your mention starts, and you can keep talking to it in that thread without mentioning it again. To stop a reply partway, react with ❌ to the agent's reply. The first time a teammate sends the agent a direct message, it replies with a card asking them to link their own Construct account.

## What result should you get?

After the first scheduled morning you should have four things, each one a place to check if something looks wrong:

1. A dated briefing in Files/briefings/, so last Tuesday's is one click away.
2. The briefing posted in #daily as an email from the agent's address.
3. A sent copy in the agent's Email app.
4. A history entry on the Calendar event, linked to the chat session the run used.

Because each day's briefing is saved as a file, the agent can answer "what did Monday's briefing say about the Acme renewal?" in Slack later in the week. If you need to trace what a run read or sent, Activity records each action; our guide to [what an agent's activity log should show](/blog/ai-agent-activity-log/) explains what it keeps.

**Illustrative example.** A three-person agency runs one briefing for the whole team. At 7:45 it lands in #daily with today's client calls and the replies waiting on each person. The founder also uses Slack's free plan for a side project, so there she uses the second route: she sends "@Construct brief me" from her phone and gets the same summary in a thread.

### How to check it in the first week

Run this checklist yourself before you rely on the briefing:

- Note the time each briefing arrives in Slack for five weekdays, and compare it with 7:45.
- Count the words in each one and check it stayed under 200.
- Compare the meeting list with your calendar and the reply list with your inbox on at least two mornings.
- Look for missed or duplicate posts, and check the Calendar event's history on any morning that looks off.
- Ask the agent in Slack about an earlier briefing to confirm the saved files are being used.

## What about Slack's own Today view?

Slack's Today view is a personal briefing inside Slack, not a team briefing you can schedule into a channel. Slack describes it as a personalized daily briefing of your priorities, calendar, and most pressing tasks, built from your own Slack activity and a connected Google or Outlook calendar ([Slack, May 6, 2026](https://slack.com/blog/news/today-daily-briefing)). Its May 2026 rollout list covers Business+ and Enterprise plans, not Pro or free ([Slack feature drop, May 29, 2026](https://slack.com/blog/news/feature-drop-may2026)). Reading your Gmail or your workspace files is not documented in the sources we reviewed.

Construct's briefing works on any Slack plan through the on-demand route, posts one shared edition to a channel on paid plans, reads your inbox and files, and keeps every edition as a file. If you already have Today, the two do not conflict.

## Troubleshooting

**The agent does not answer in Slack.** Check that you mentioned it (in a channel, plain messages outside its threads are ignored), that you are on the Full access list, and, for a private channel, that the bot was invited. If it is silent for everyone, disconnect it and add it again from Settings, Messaging.

**The briefing email never appeared in the channel.** Confirm the address in the prompt matches the one Slack gave you, that your Slack plan is paid, and that your admin has not turned off channel email addresses. Then press **Run now** on the event.

**The briefing posted twice.** Keep the "if today's briefing file already exists, do not send it again" line in the scheduled prompt.

**It arrived at the wrong time.** The event keeps the time zone it was created with. Edit the event's time zone, not just your profile.

**A guest got a thin answer.** People outside the allow list get a read-only agent with no connected apps. Add them to **Full access** if they should see the full briefing.

**It flagged too much email as urgent.** Tighten the rule in step 4, or move the inbox sorting into its own job; our [inbox triage guide](/blog/ai-inbox-triage/) sets up Reply needed and FYI labels the briefing can read.
