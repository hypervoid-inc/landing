**Meta's Muse for Small Business costs nothing to start, with paid Muse plans at $20 and $100 a month, so the real question for most owners is trust and fit, not price.** If you want an agent for work outside Meta's apps, the shortlist is Construct (from $9 a month) for recurring jobs in a workspace you control, Claude Cowork or Gemini Spark (around $20) for one person's work, OpenAI Dots (with ChatGPT Pro from $100) for an always-on agent, Lindy or Viktor for teams in Slack, and Sintra or Marblism for fixed roles like social media or answering the phone.

This page is about Meta's Muse agent, not other products that share the Muse name. We build Construct, one of the nine alternatives below, and we say where the others are the better choice.

## What is Meta's Muse for Small Business?

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent. Meta [announced it on September 8, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) and describes it as running on "Muse Secure VM, a dedicated, virtual machine (VM) that houses both the agent and a person's data." A separate agent, which Meta calls Sentinel, checks what leaves that machine, and Muse asks for approval before it sends email or makes a purchase.

It spread fast. In its first 12 days, Muse reached 2.8 million global installs and passed ChatGPT for daily users on iOS in the US and Canada, according to [TechCrunch's analysis of app data](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-muse-is-outpacing-chatgpts-early-mobile-launch/). The same report found that "over 95% of Muse's users are also Facebook users."

On September 29, Meta launched [Muse for Small Business](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-small-business/). It connects the same agent to business tools. Meta's announcement names Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom, plus Facebook and Instagram business accounts and custom connectors. [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/meta-puts-popular-new-tool-hands-small-businesses) adds Meta ad accounts and describes jobs such as drafting ad campaigns, writing a week of posts, and building a growth plan from sales and social data. It is available in the US and Canada.

Meta's pitch is direct. "They told us they're short on hours, not ideas," the company said of small business owners, as reported by [CNBC Africa](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/2026/meta-launches-muse-for-small-business-as-zuckerberg-pushes-beyond-consumer-ai-market), which also cites 200 million small businesses on Facebook.

## What is Muse for Small Business good at?

Before looking elsewhere, it is worth being clear about what Muse does well, because for some businesses it will be the right answer.

- **Price.** Meta says Muse "is free for most of what people need." The business version uses the same pricing as the consumer app.
- **The Meta ecosystem.** If your customers find you on Instagram and Facebook and your ads run through Meta, Muse can read your page analytics and ad accounts directly. No other agent on this list has that first-party access.
- **Commerce tools out of the box.** Shopify, QuickBooks, Stripe, and Klaviyo are named launch connectors, which covers a lot of a small online store's stack.
- **A security design you can read about.** The per-user VM, the Sentinel approval layer, and the rule that "nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval" are documented in Meta's own announcements.
- **Reach.** It works in the iOS and Android apps, on the web, and inside WhatsApp, according to [Meta's launch post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/).

If most of your marketing and selling happens on Meta's apps, try Muse first. It is free to start, so testing it costs you only setup time.

## Why do small businesses look for a Muse alternative?

The reasons we see fall into four groups. None of them mean Muse is a poor product. They are about fit.

### Trusting Meta with business data

Giving an agent access to your books, store, and inbox is a bigger step than giving it your Instagram login. Meta says "Muse doesn't share a person's conversations or the data in their VM with Meta's ad systems," that users can opt out of their interactions being used to train Meta's models, and that a Confidential VM encrypted with a key only you hold is coming. Some owners will find that enough. Others will not.

Ebrahim Bagheri of the University of Toronto's Faculty of Information put the tradeoff plainly to [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12077192/metas-ai-agent-muse-privacy/): "The more you want accomplish with agents, the more you have to give away your access to your data." That applies to every product on this list, including ours. The question is which company you want holding that access.

### Websites that block AI agents

On September 21, Amazon blocked Muse from buying on Amazon.com. Users saw a message that "Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use," [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-ai-agent-has-been-blocked-from-using-amazon-com/). That was about shopping, but the lesson carries over to business work: any agent that clicks through websites can be refused by a site. Switching agents does not remove that risk. Leaning on official integrations and APIs, where they exist, does reduce it.

### Staying inside Meta's apps

Muse lives in your Meta account and Meta's apps. That is convenient if your team already works there and limiting if it works in Slack, Teams, or email. Availability is also narrow for now: Muse for Small Business is US and Canada only, and [Meta's subscription help page](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/) says "Muse and Muse subscriptions are in limited testing and aren't available in all locations yet."

### Knowing what a busy month costs

Muse's paid plans are metered in tokens per week, which is hard to translate into jobs done. [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/muse-agent) also reports that Muse asks for a payment card even on the free tier. For a business that wants to budget a fixed monthly amount, that can be a reason to compare.

## Agents from the big AI labs

These are the closest to Muse in shape: one company's general agent, bundled with a subscription you may already pay for.

### OpenAI Dots

**Best for:** people who already live in ChatGPT and want an agent that keeps working while they are away.

OpenAI unveiled Dots on September 29, 2026, the same day as Muse for Small Business. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sam-altman-openai-dots-chatgpt-agents-safety/) reports they run on GPT-6 Astra, work inside ChatGPT with Slack and Teams channels, and handle jobs like scheduling meetings, budgeting, and travel. [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/blog/posts/openai-dots-guide), summarizing OpenAI's documentation, says each Dot has "their own cloud computer and browser."

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** your first Dot is included with ChatGPT Pro, which starts at $100 a month, or with a Business Premium seat. The sources disagree on that seat: CBS lists Business Premium at $20 a month, while [BenchLM's Dots guide](https://benchlm.ai/blog/posts/openai-dots-guide) lists $125 per seat monthly or $100 billed annually. **Watch for:** it has the highest entry price on this list. BenchLM reports that Pro users in the EEA, Switzerland, and the UK were excluded at launch and that the price of additional Dots has not been published.

### Claude Cowork

**Best for:** one person's research, writing, and file work, handed off and picked up later.

Anthropic describes Cowork as a way to "describe an outcome, step away, and come back to finished work." Its [getting started guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-claude-cowork) says tasks can run "remotely in the cloud (in beta)," scheduled tasks "don't need your computer to be awake," and each connector can be set to always allow, needs approval, or blocked.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** included with Claude Pro at $20 a month, or $17 a month billed annually, per [Claude's pricing page](https://claude.com/pricing). Max starts at $100. Team seats are $25 a month, or $125 for a Premium seat. The pricing page now says "Claude Cowork is now just Claude," so it is part of the main app rather than a separate product.

**Watch for:** it is not on the free plan, and heavy multi-step jobs use up plan limits faster. Individual plans are built around one person's work; a team needs Team seats.

### Gemini Spark

**Best for:** a solo owner who runs the business from a personal Gmail, Calendar, and Drive.

[Google's help page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en) describes Spark as an agent that runs scheduled tasks, browses in your own Chrome or a remote browser, and works across Gmail, Calendar, Docs, Drive, Sheets, and other Google apps. It asks before sending messages, changing data, making purchases, or signing into websites.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** included with Google AI Pro or Ultra. [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/googles-agentic-ai-gemini-spark-is-now-available-on-the-20-pro-tier-in-the-us/) reported in July that Spark came to the $20 Pro tier in the US after starting on Ultra. Google's plan pages showed us regional prices only. {/* TODO: verify exact US Google AI Pro price on a Google page */}

**Watch for:** Google says Spark "isn't available if you sign in with a work or school Google Account." If your business runs on Google Workspace, that rules it out for now. It is also unavailable in the EEA, Nigeria, Switzerland, and the UK.

### Grok Bot

**Best for:** people who already pay for Cursor.

Grok Bot gives you Bots that share one persistent cloud computer. Its [computer and apps documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps) says all Bots on an account share the same browser, files, and signed-in accounts, with a separate screen for each Bot. It prefers connectors from its Marketplace over clicking through websites.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** included with [Cursor Individual at $20 a month](https://cursor.com/pricing) and Cursor Teams at $40 per user. The Grok Bot product page did not load for us. {/* TODO: verify SuperGrok access and price, and the apps Grok Bot runs in, on x.ai/bot */}

## A workspace built for recurring business work

### Construct

**Best for:** solo founders and small teams whose work crosses several apps and repeats every week.

Construct is an AI employee that works from a persistent cloud workspace. Each agent has files, live browser runs, a sandbox terminal, its own email address, a Calendar of scheduled jobs, and connected apps through a live integration catalog that includes Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable. When a connector does not exist, you can add a custom MCP tool. You reach it from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord slash commands, or by emailing it.

Where it differs from Muse is where the work lives. Finished work lands in the workspace as it is produced, so a run that fails at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29, and the next run starts from what exists. Memory can be inspected, corrected, or forgotten. For a repeated internal process, Construct can build a private workspace app, as our [internal tools guide](/blog/build-internal-tools-with-construct/) explains.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Lite is $9 a month for 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Starter is $59 with 6x Lite's usage, up to 5 agents, 150 steps per task, and 10 scheduled tasks. Pro is $299 with 32x Lite's usage, up to 15 agents, up to 1,000 steps per task, 50 scheduled tasks, and bring-your-own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. Pro has a 7-day trial. See [Construct pricing](/pricing/).

**Watch for:** there is no free tier, so Muse is cheaper to try. Construct does not have Meta's first-party access to its own ad accounts and Instagram analytics. {/* TODO: confirm whether Shopify, QuickBooks, Stripe, and Meta business accounts are in Construct's live integration catalog, and name them here if so */} Workflows are linear today, with no branching, delays, or approval gates. Activity summaries and tool records are useful operating records, not a complete audit log. There is no built-in phone agent. And like any agent that uses a browser, it can be refused by sites that block agents.

## AI teammates in Slack or Teams

If your team already coordinates in chat, an agent that lives there avoids adding another app.

### Lindy

**Best for:** teams whose work already happens in Slack.

Lindy's product is a Slack-native teammate. Its [pricing page](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing) lists scheduled routines, meeting notes, inbox management, computer use, and integrations on every plan, with a credit pool shared across the team.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Plus is $29.99 per user a month with 3,000 credits, Pro is $99.99 with 15,000, and Max is $199.99 with 35,000. Enterprise adds HIPAA compliance and audit logs. There is a 7-day trial.

**Watch for:** it is billed per user, so cost grows with headcount. Unused credits do not roll over. Our [roundup of AI employee platforms](/blog/best-ai-employee-platforms/) covers Lindy's seat rules in more detail.

### Viktor

**Best for:** a Slack or Teams workspace that wants the agent to build small tools and dashboards, not only answer.

Viktor describes itself as an AI employee "that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams" with "his own computer in the cloud where he writes and runs code." Its [homepage](https://viktor.com/) lists more than 3,200 integrations, including Stripe and HubSpot, and says it asks for confirmation "before high-stakes actions like sending emails or deploying to production." Viktor says it is SOC 2 Type 1 compliant.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** you start with $100 in free credits and no card. The first Team plan is $50 a month for 20,000 credits, and [Viktor's pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing) is per workspace rather than per seat. Unused plan credits roll over for one month.

**Watch for:** credit use varies with the job, so estimate from a few real tasks before committing. It only lives in Slack or Teams, so it is less useful if your team coordinates elsewhere.

## Role-based AI staff

These give you named AI employees with narrow jobs. They are the quickest to start when your need matches a role.

### Sintra

**Best for:** a solo owner who wants help with social media, copy, email, and support without setting anything up.

Sintra sells 12 role-based AI helpers, such as Soshie for social media, Cassie for customer support, and Vizzy as a virtual assistant, that share one memory it calls Brain AI. Its [homepage](https://sintra.ai/) says it connects with Facebook, Instagram, Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Google Drive, Notion, and more, and runs on the web and in iOS and Android apps.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** the Sintra X bundle lists at $97 a month and was on sale at $48.50 for a one-month term when we checked, with lower monthly rates on 3- and 12-month terms. Each plan includes 250 credits a month and a 14-day money-back guarantee. Single helpers are $39 a month each, per [Sintra's pricing page](https://sintra.ai/pricing).

**Watch for:** sale prices change, so check the term you are actually buying. Each helper does one role well, and work that crosses roles or needs a custom process is a weaker fit.

### Marblism

**Best for:** a local business that needs calls answered, plus inbox and social help.

Marblism's seven AI employees include Rachel, a receptionist who answers calls around the clock and books appointments, Sonny for social posts across Instagram, LinkedIn, X, and Facebook, and Eva for email and calendar. Nothing else on this list answers your phone.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** $44 a month, or $24 a month billed yearly, for all seven employees, 50 hours of work a month, and unlimited team members, per [Marblism's pricing page](https://www.marblism.com/pricing).

**Watch for:** work is metered in hours, and each task type uses a set amount. If most of your work is not one of the seven roles, a general agent fits better.

## A worked example: the Monday sales and ads summary

This is an illustrative workflow, not a customer case study or a measured test.

A two-person online store wants a Monday summary: last week's orders and refunds, which products sold, what the Instagram ads spent, and three things to do this week.

**On Muse for Small Business**, this fits naturally. Connect Shopify, Stripe, and the Meta ad account, then ask for the summary. Muse can read the ad data directly, which is its biggest advantage for this job. You approve anything it wants to publish or send.

**On Construct**, you would save the report format and last week's report in the workspace, connect the apps it needs (or upload an order export if a connector is not available), and ask the agent to compare this week against last week and save a dated file with its sources. After a few good runs, save it as a workflow and put it on the Calendar, which needs Starter for more than 3 scheduled tasks. The report history stays in Files, so each week starts from the last one. The ad spend part is where Construct is weaker: unless Meta ads are available as a connected app, you would supply an export.

**On a role-based tool** such as Sintra, you would split this across helpers and assemble the result yourself.

Judge the result by whether the numbers match your store, whether each figure is traceable, and how many minutes of editing it takes. Those are better buying criteria than how impressive the first run feels.

## Which Muse alternative should you choose?

- **If most of your selling and advertising happens on Instagram and Facebook,** start with Muse for Small Business. It is free to try and has access nothing else here has.
- **If you want recurring jobs across several apps, with files, schedules, and memory you can inspect in one workspace,** choose Construct, starting at $9.
- **If you already pay for ChatGPT Pro,** try your included Dot before buying anything else.
- **If you are one person doing research and documents,** Claude Cowork on Claude Pro at $20 is the simplest start.
- **If your business runs on a personal Gmail account,** Gemini Spark on Google AI Pro fits. If you use Google Workspace, it does not, for now.
- **If you already pay for Cursor,** Grok Bot is included.
- **If your team works in Slack,** compare Lindy (per user) with Viktor (per workspace) on what five people would actually cost.
- **If you need the phone answered,** Marblism is the only option here that does it.
- **If you want marketing output without setup,** Sintra's bundle covers the common roles.
- **If you are outside the US and Canada,** Muse for Small Business is not available yet, and Gemini Spark and Dots on Pro exclude several European markets. Check availability before comparing features.

Whatever you pick, start with one job whose output you can check, keep payments and customer messages behind your approval, and prefer an official integration over browser clicks where one exists.
