## What is the difference between an activity log and an audit log?

An activity log is a short, readable record for the person who delegated the work; an audit log is a complete record that lets someone else reconstruct exactly what happened. The first is built for speed, the second for proof.

An activity log answers practical questions quickly: what ran, what it touched, whether it worked, and roughly why it happened. It is short on purpose, because nobody reads a feed of raw tool payloads.

An audit log is written for someone who has to reconstruct events later, often someone who does not trust the system that wrote it. NIST's glossary defines an audit trail as "a chronological record that reconstructs and examines the sequence of activities surrounding or leading to a specific operation, procedure, or event in a security-relevant transaction from inception to result" ([NIST CSRC glossary, citing SP 800-53 Rev. 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/audit_trail)). The words that matter are "reconstructs" and "from inception to result."

<ArticleTable caption="Activity log vs audit log for an AI agent">

| Property            | Activity log                            | Audit log                                           |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Main reader         | The person who delegated the work       | A reviewer, auditor, or investigator                |
| Question it answers | What did the agent do, and roughly why? | Can we reconstruct exactly what happened?           |
| Detail              | One readable line per action            | Inputs, outputs, identities, timestamps             |
| Completeness        | Best effort is acceptable               | Gaps are a defect                                   |
| Retention           | Weeks is usually enough                 | Set by policy or regulation, often years            |
| Tamper resistance   | Not required                            | Usually required (append-only, signed, or exported) |

</ArticleTable>

A small team needs the first column every day. Construct's Activity is built for exactly that, and the rest of the workspace holds the detail behind each row.

## What does Construct's AI agent activity log record?

Activity writes one row every time an agent calls a tool, with what happened, why, whether it worked, and a link to the chat. It is a desktop app in your Construct workspace, and each row carries:

- **Kind:** filterable as Actions, Files, Calendar, Workflows, Delegation, or Errors.
- **What happened:** a headline built from the agent's name, a verb, and the target, such as the search query, the URL it fetched, or the goal it gave the browser.
- **Why:** a reason of up to 160 characters. When the agent skips that sentence, the row stores only the what.
- **Status:** OK, Failed, or Skipped.
- **Where:** which agent, and a link back to the chat the action came from.

You can search the feed, filter it by kind, status, and date range (today, the last 7 days, the last 30 days, or everything still kept), and export the filtered view as a CSV for a spreadsheet or as JSON. Each export holds up to 50,000 rows.

Chats stay until you delete them. Workflow and calendar history stay with the workflow or calendar item. Files stay until you delete them or close your account. So a row that has aged out of Activity often still has its evidence in one of these places.

## Before you start: Activity is an operating record

Activity is built to be read, not to satisfy an auditor, so set it up with that in mind:

- **It keeps a rolling window.** Rows older than 30 days are deleted, and the oldest go first once a person's feed passes about 10 MB. Export the CSV each week if you want a longer history.
- **It summarizes rather than copies.** Rows do not store tool inputs or outputs, and writing a row never fails the job it describes, so a row can occasionally be missing. For the full detail, open the linked chat, Terminal, or Files.
- **Each person sees their own feed.** Activity rows belong to the person whose agent acted, so a team workspace has no single feed of every member's agent actions. For jobs that matter to the team, add a final step: "Append one line to `Files/logs/agent-runs.csv` with the date, what you did, and links to what you produced." That file lives in the workspace as long as you keep it.
- **Workflows do not pause for approval yet.** For payments or customer email, ask for drafts and send them yourself; the [verification gap post](/blog/agent-verification-gap/) explains why that review is where the real time goes.

If your compliance team needs a tamper-evident, complete record of every agent operation, Activity is not that record. Keep those actions in a system of record that is, such as your CRM's record history or your accounting tool's change log, and use Construct's exports and run-log files alongside it.

## How do you trace an AI agent's action back to its cause?

Start from the Activity row, open the chat it links to, then check the memory, the schedule, and what went out. This example is illustrative, not a customer case, and every step uses records Construct already keeps.

A four-person design studio runs a scheduled Construct job each weekday at 9am that checks the agent's inbox for supplier quotes, saves them to Files, and sends the owner a summary. On Thursday, a supplier replies to an email the owner does not remember approving.

1. **Start in Activity.** Filter to the last 7 days and search the supplier's name. One row says the agent replied to the supplier's thread, with the reason "Supplier asked for delivery date; standing rule says confirm dates."
2. **Open the chat.** The row links to the run. The transcript shows the agent read the quote, checked a memory, and used it to decide that a delivery-date question was safe to answer.
3. **Check the memory.** In Memories, the rule "confirm delivery dates with suppliers" exists, with the conversation it was learned from. It was meant for one supplier, not all of them. Correct it, or forget it.
4. **Check the schedule.** The Calendar event's **Recent fires** shows the 9am occurrence ran and succeeded, so this was the scheduled job, not someone's ad hoc request.
5. **Read what went out.** The Sent folder has the reply. It was harmless this time.
6. **Fix the job, not just the record.** Change the scheduled prompt so supplier replies are drafted, not sent, and export that week's Activity as a CSV if you want a copy in your own records.

The fix in step 3 changes every later run, not just this one. Our guide to [letting an agent use Gmail safely](/blog/ai-agent-gmail-access-safely/) covers the drafting rule in more detail, and [inbox triage with drafts, not sends](/blog/ai-inbox-triage/) applies the same pattern to a busier mailbox.

## What should an AI agent audit trail record for a small team?

Five records cover almost every question a small team asks after the fact: the action, the conversation behind it, the artifact, the schedule history, and the belief the agent acted on. Construct keeps all five:

1. **One line per action, with a reason.** Enough to scan a week in a minute. That is Activity.
2. **The conversation behind it.** The instruction the agent was following and the answers it got. Every Activity row links to its chat.
3. **The artifacts.** The file, the sent message, the updated record. Construct saves them to Files as they are produced; see [how a persistent workspace keeps finished work](/blog/ai-agent-with-its-own-computer/).
4. **The schedule history.** Whether a recurring job ran, skipped, or failed. Construct keeps this per calendar item and per workflow run, as our guide to [running an agent on a schedule](/blog/ai-agent-scheduled-tasks/) shows.
5. **The beliefs.** What the agent remembered and from where, so a wrong rule can be fixed at the source. That is what [inspectable memory](/use-cases/memory/) is for.

## How do you audit an AI agent product's records before you buy?

Run each of these as a test on a trial account, not as a question on a sales call.

1. **Can you find yesterday's actions in under a minute?** Search and filters beat a long scroll.
2. **Does each action say why, not just what?** A reason is what turns a log into an explanation.
3. **Can you jump from an action to the conversation behind it?** Without that link, you are reconstructing context by hand.
4. **Are inputs and outputs kept, and for how long?** Ask for the number of days, not "we keep logs."
5. **Can you export it?** CSV or JSON you can store yourself is the minimum for anything you might need later.
6. **Who can see it?** Per person, per workspace, or an admin-wide view.
7. **Is it ever pruned or rewritten?** Rolling retention is fine for operations and wrong for audits.
8. **Does the record survive a failed run?** A job that dies halfway should still show what it did before it stopped.

Construct answers yes to 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8. It keeps Activity rows for 30 days with the detail in the linked chat, Terminal, and Files, and shows each person their own feed.
