Hiring a virtual assistant used to mean one decision: which agency or freelancer. Now it means a second one first - human VA, or a supervised AI agent for suitable parts of the job at a fraction of the monthly bill. This post puts real numbers next to each option, concedes where the human still wins, and explains how inspectable work records improve supervision beyond a chat-based "AI VA."

## What a virtual assistant does vs what an AI employee does

A human virtual assistant handles inbox triage, calendar management, data entry, light research, travel booking, and customer or client communication. The value is judgment applied to a known playbook: a good VA learns your preferences, catches exceptions, and represents you in conversations that require tone and tact.

An **AI employee** takes on many of the same tasks but executes them directly inside available tools instead of describing the steps back to you. For a scheduled inbox-review job, Construct can read and search its native inbox, draft or send replies, and report uncertain cases for a human decision. Connected Gmail actions may provide additional organization features when available. The Activity feed records bounded action summaries and best-effort reasons, while chat retains bounded tool records. If you have not seen the term before, [what is an AI employee](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee/) covers the concept in full.

It is worth separating this from a plain chat assistant, too. A tool that only answers questions in a chat window is not doing a VA's job, no matter how good the answers are; see [chat assistants vs AI employees](/blog/chat-assistants-vs-ai-employees/) if that is the comparison you actually meant to make. And if the "VA" work you are trying to replace is really a fixed sequence of triggers and actions rather than judgment calls, a deterministic automation platform may fit better than either option here, which is the case made in [Construct vs Zapier](/blog/construct-vs-zapier/).

## Cost comparison: monthly VA spend vs a supervised AI agent

### What a human VA actually costs in 2026

Human VA pricing varies by where the person is based and how the engagement is structured. Hourly rates run $5 to $75 depending on location and skill level, and a part-time arrangement covering daily email and calendar management typically runs $1,000 to $3,000 a month, according to Tendem's breakdown of virtual assistant costs (source: [tendem.ai](https://tendem.ai/blog/virtual-assistant-vs-ai-agent-which-do-you-need)).

Full-time engagements show a wide domestic-versus-offshore split. Cherry Assistant puts an offshore managed agency at $640 to $2,400 a month full-time, against $4,700 to $8,300 a month as the realistic total cost of a US-based in-house assistant once salary, benefits, payroll taxes, and recruiting are included (source: [cherryassistant.com](https://www.cherryassistant.com/blog/how-much-does-a-virtual-assistant-cost)). Stealth Agents' research on Filipino VA agencies lands in a similar range: $700 to $2,500 a month all-in for a managed agency hire, versus $4,800 to $12,000 a month for a comparable US-based hire (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-a-virtual-assistant-in-the-philippines-2026)).

Two more numbers matter beyond the sticker price. A structured offshore executive VA onboarding typically reaches full productivity between day 14 and day 30, and a rushed one can take 60 to 90 days or end in early turnover (source: [outsourcedscale.com](https://www.outsourcedscale.com/blog/how-to-onboard-an-offshore-executive-virtual-assistant-so-they-perform-from-day-one/)). Once someone is up to speed, outsourced VA teams still see about 18% annual turnover, and each replacement costs an estimated $3,600 to $5,400 in lost productivity and rehiring (source: [365outsource.com](https://www.365outsource.com/virtual-assistance/virtual-assistant-retention-hiring-strategy/)).

### Where the gap comes from, and where it narrows

Customer-service benchmarks illustrate part of the structural cost difference. The cited analysis estimates a routine AI-handled customer interaction at under $0.50, against $4 to $8 for a human agent (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-virtual-assistant-vs-human-virtual-assistant-statistics-2026)). Those figures do not establish the cost of unrelated VA work, but they show why narrow, repeatable interactions are commonly considered for automation first.

The gap narrows on anything that needs relationship context, negotiation, or judgment calls a playbook cannot fully specify, which is exactly the work covered in the next section. Cost-per-interaction estimates should not be read as evidence that a supervised agent can replace the full scope of a human VA role.

## Where a human VA still wins

Concede this plainly: for customer-service interactions that depend on tone, empathy, or reading a client's mood, people still report a strong preference for human help. The cited analysis reports 88% satisfaction for human-handled customer-service interactions against 60% for AI-handled ones, while 93.4% of US consumers say they prefer a human for customer service work (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-virtual-assistant-vs-human-virtual-assistant-statistics-2026)).

That preference tracks with how the executive assistant role is actually evolving, not disappearing. As Oana Manolache, founder and CEO of Sequel.io, put it: "it doesn't replace what an executive assistant does now as the role has evolved" (source: [fortune.com](https://fortune.com/2026/07/05/ai-displacement-executive-assistants-secretaries-skills-adaptation/)). High-stakes client calls, sensitive HR conversations, in-person coordination, and any relationship where the other party specifically wants to be talking to a person are still a human VA's territory, and no pricing table changes that.

## Where a supervised AI agent wins: speed, availability, and inspectable work

A supervised AI agent can run scheduled work while you are offline and does not carry employee turnover or replacement cost. Construct's inspectable memory can retain selected context across tasks, but it is not a complete record of institutional knowledge and does not automatically index files, private app results, terminal output, or live browser runs; see [AI agent memory](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) for how it works.

The wedge a chat-based "AI VA" cannot match is an inspectable work record. A chatbot that drafts a reply for you to copy and send does not itself record what happened in your inbox, calendar, or CRM. Construct's Activity feed keeps bounded action summaries and best-effort reasons across its tools, while chat stores bounded tool records and resulting files or messages remain in the workspace. Activity is a rolling product-observability feed, not a forensic audit log.

This is also a realistic moment to size the category correctly rather than oversell it. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey found that no more than 10% of organizations report scaling agentic AI within any single business function, while 23% are actively scaling in at least one function and 39% are still experimenting (source: [forbes.com](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/22/10-of-enterprise-functions-use-ai-agents-mckinsey-finds/)). Supervised, bounded use on specific recurring tasks is where the category actually is in 2026, not full autonomous replacement of a VA's entire job.

## Choosing between the two, or running both together

Match the tool to the task rather than the category. Recurring, cross-app work with a clear procedure is where a supervised AI agent's cost and speed advantage is largest: inbox review, CRM updates, scheduled research pulls, status reporting. That is also where inspectable outputs and action summaries matter most. Client-facing judgment calls, sensitive conversations, and anything that depends on a relationship built over time are still better handled by a person.

Many teams end up running both: a human VA for relationship-dependent work, and an AI employee handling the recurring execution around it. If you want to see what that looks like day to day, start with [what is an AI employee](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee/), or compare the underlying execution model in [Construct vs ChatGPT](/blog/construct-vs-chatgpt/) if you are still deciding whether you need an agent or a chat assistant at all.
