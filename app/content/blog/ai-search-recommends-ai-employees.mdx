## Why this matters for anyone buying an AI employee

More buyers now take the AI answer as the research. Pew Research Center found that Google users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits, against 15% for users who did not see one, and clicked a link inside the summary itself in just 1% of visits ([Pew Research Center, July 22, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). If the answer is where the shortlist gets made, it is worth knowing how that shortlist is built.

An AI engine does not test the products it recommends. It summarises what has been written about them, weighted by whatever its search step found. So a recommendation tells you which products have the most, and the most findable, writing behind them. That can line up with which product is best for your job. It does not have to.

**Disclosure:** we build Construct, which is one of the products in this category. That is why we ran this study, and it is why we publish the full method, every prompt, and Construct's own results in the same tables as everyone else's.

### Errors, retries, and follow-ups

- **Engine errors.** A run that hit an engine's own error message was retried once after that engine finished its other prompts. Runs that failed again are reported as errors and left out of every share below.
- **Message limits.** If an engine ran out of free messages or failed three times in a row, only that engine paused. We resumed it the same day.
- **Clarifying questions.** Some engines answer a broad question with a question of their own. When an answer was short (under about 700 characters), ended with a question, and cited no sources, the script sent one standard reply, "A generalist that can run multi-step tasks.", and scored the second answer. The reply is identical on every engine and every date.

### What counts as "named", "recommended", and "cited"

- **Named:** the product appears anywhere in the answer, other than in a repeat of the prompt's own words.
- **Recommended:** the answer presents the product as an option to use, for example in a list of suggestions or as the direct answer. A product mentioned only as a contrast ("unlike Zapier, this...") or in a warning is named but not recommended. Every product count in this report is a recommendation count unless a table says otherwise.
- **Products named in the prompt don't count for that prompt.** "Lindy alternatives" will mention Lindy; we do not credit Lindy for it.
- **Brand prompts are excluded** from product rankings. They ask about Construct by name, so they say nothing about which products the engines reach for unprompted. That leaves 50 prompts per engine per audit for the rankings.
- **First-mention rank:** the position of the product among the recommended products in that answer, in reading order. Rank 1 is the first product recommended.
- **Cited:** a link in the answer's sources or inline citations that points outside the engine. The engine's own pages, Google redirect links, and tracking parameters are removed, and each link is reduced to its domain for counting.

### How products were extracted

The audit script records whether Construct was named and which links were cited. Recommended products were extracted from the saved answer text in a second pass:

1. **Dictionary match.** A list of [n] products in or near the category, each with its common spellings and domains (for example "Lindy" and "lindy.ai"), matched against every answer. {/* TODO: data. Publish the dictionary as part of the CSV. */}
2. **Unmatched names.** Every capitalised product-like name not in the dictionary was listed for review and added if it was a product.
3. **Recommended or only mentioned.** Two people coded each match as recommended or mentioned using the definition above. Disagreements were settled together and logged. {/* TODO: data. Report the number of disagreements and the agreement rate. */}
4. **Construct's mentions** were checked by hand, because the word "construct" is also an ordinary English word. Every brand prompt answer was read in full to confirm it described this Construct and not something else with the same name.

## Limitations

- **One answer per prompt, per engine, per date.** AI answers vary between runs. A product appearing in one answer and not the next can be chance. {/* TODO: data. If time allows, rerun 10 prompts five times on each engine and report how stable the top five is. */}
- **Signed-in accounts.** Five of the six engines ran in signed-in private modes. We turned off memory and personalisation where the engine allows it, but we cannot rule out account-level effects.
- **Our prompts.** We wrote them, and some reflect how we describe the category. We mark those and report with and without them.
- **Default models change.** Engines update their default models without notice, so a change between audits can come from the model rather than from the web.
- **Location.** All audits ran from [location], and engines localise answers. {/* TODO: data */}
- **We are in the category.** We applied every rule to Construct exactly as to every other product, and the full data is public so you can check.

## What this means if you are choosing an agent

{/* TODO: data. Keep the points the data supports; rewrite or delete the rest. */}

**A recommendation reflects what has been written, not what was tested.** No engine in this study ran the products it recommended. If the most-cited pages are listicles, the shortlist you get is roughly the consensus of those listicles, including the ones written by vendors about their own category.

**Ask two engines, not one.** If the engines disagree as much as [finding], a single engine's answer is one view of the market, not the market. {/* TODO: data */}

**Open the sources.** An answer that cites a vendor's own "best tools" article is repeating that vendor's framing. Check who published the page before you trust its ranking.

**Check prices and limits on the vendor's own page.** AI answers can repeat old prices from cached articles. Plans in this category changed often in 2026, so confirm the current price, usage limits, and what the entry plan actually includes before you buy.

**Run your own job.** The fastest test of any AI employee is a real task you do every week. Give it the same inputs on two products, read the saved result, and count how long it took you to check it. Our [AI delegation checklist](/blog/ai-delegation-checklist/) gives you a structure for that trial.

### Where Construct fits

An AI answer can tell you which names come up. It cannot tell you whether a product will finish your Monday report. These are the parts of Construct you can check for yourself in a first week, without relying on anyone's summary:

- **The price and limits are on one page.** Lite is $9 a month with 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Starter is $59 and Pro is $299, with every limit listed on the [pricing page](/pricing/).
