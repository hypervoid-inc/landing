## Why hire the AI employee, and why Construct?

- **It starts without you.** Calendar schedules run on Construct's servers with your laptop closed. Lite includes 3 scheduled tasks, Starter 10, and Pro 50.
- **It keeps last week.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts land in workspace files as the agent produces them, so each run builds on the last one's files, and a run that stops at step 30 still leaves steps 1 to 29 behind.
- **Its own identity.** Each agent has its own email address, so routine updates go out from the agent, not from you.
- **It covers coworker work too.** Hand it a one-off task in chat, watch it work in its own browser and terminal, and save the steps as a workflow once it works.
- **Shared by the team, without seats.** Team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles, no member limit, and usage that draws on the owner's plan. Reach the agent from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord, or its inbox.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Open what it has learned, correct a fact, or forget it, so it does not meet you as a stranger every Monday.

## What is the difference, in one table?

The lines blur. Lindy's documentation opens with "Lindy is an AI employee," Anthropic calls its agent mode Cowork, and Viktor describes itself as "the AI employee that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams." What matters is the row-by-row behavior, not the noun on the box.

## Which one do you need?

Answer five questions. If you say yes to the third, you need an AI employee.

### 1. Do you mostly need answers, drafts, and thinking help?

That is **AI assistant** work: an email, a contract clause, a spreadsheet formula, a pitch brainstorm, judged on the spot while you are present. You probably already pay for a chat assistant, and it stays useful for this.

### 2. Do you want to hand over a task and come back to it finished?

That is **AI coworker** work: twenty minutes to an hour of clicking, such as researching ten suppliers or filling a tracker from a pile of PDFs. Anthropic's description of Cowork captures the shape: "describe an outcome, step away, and come back to finished work" ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-claude-cowork)). Construct takes the same kind of one-off task in chat, in its own cloud browser and terminal.

### 3. Does the same job come back every week, whether or not you remember it?

This is where an **AI employee** earns its place. The Monday competitor check, the Friday client report, the daily inbox summary: the value comes from the job running without you starting it, and from each run building on the last one's files. In Construct, you run the job once in chat, save it as a workflow, and put it on the Calendar.

### 4. Does the work need its own identity or its own place?

An agent that receives forwarded email at its own address, keeps a folder of past reports, and sends routine updates from an address that is not yours is acting as an employee, not a coworker in your accounts. A separate identity also limits the damage if something goes wrong ([why give an agent its own email address](/blog/ai-agent-email-address/)).

### 5. Will more than one person hand it work, or pick up its results?

Then you need something built for shared use: a workspace with roles, shared files, and a channel the team already uses. A personal assistant with extra seats is still a set of private threads ([chat assistants vs AI employees](/blog/chat-assistants-vs-ai-employees/)). Construct's team workspaces share the same agent, files, and history without a per-seat fee.

**The short answer:** keep the chat assistant you already have for quick answers, and hire an AI employee for the first job that recurs. Because Construct also handles one-off tasks, most small teams do not need a separate coworker product on top.

## One job, three ways

Take a single job: a weekly summary of what five competitors changed on their pricing pages. This is illustrative, not a measured test.

**With an assistant.** On Monday you remember, paste five URLs into chat, and ask for a comparison with last week. It cannot see last week unless you paste that too. You copy the answer into a doc. Ten minutes if you remember; zero if you forget.

**With a coworker.** You ask it to open the five pages, compare them with last week's notes in your Drive, and update the doc. It does the clicking while you do something else, asks before editing the shared doc, and you check the result. Still your job to start it on Monday.

**With an AI employee such as Construct.** The job runs at 7am every Monday. The agent opens the five pages, saves a dated copy of each, compares them with last week's saved copies in its own files, writes the summary, and emails it to the team from its own address. On Monday you read a finished summary. When a competitor redesigns a page and the comparison gets worse, the run history shows which week it started.

The employee version is not smarter. It has three things the others lack for this job: a schedule, a place to keep last week, and an identity to deliver from.

## How do you hire your first AI employee in Construct?

Here is the Monday competitor summary from above, set up in Construct.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial.**
2. **Save the five competitor URLs** in a workspace file, and ask the agent in chat to open each pricing page, save a dated copy, and write a summary of what changed.
3. **Check the first result in Files.** Correct anything it got wrong; the agent saves your corrections as memory you can read.
4. **Save the steps as a workflow.** After a task works, Construct can offer to save it as a workflow draft.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** for 7am every Monday, using one of Lite's 3 scheduled tasks, and have it email the summary to the team from its own address.

Already using ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini? Keep it for quick answers. There is no import, so copy the instructions you keep pasting into chat into your first Construct request, and move the files the job needs into the workspace. The two work side by side.

### Before you start

- **Keep a person on anything irreversible.** An AI employee does not replace judgment about people, money, or clients. Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so for anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them.
- **Keep jobs short.** Reliability falls as jobs get longer: our [agent task half-life](/blog/agent-task-half-life/) piece shows why a 48-step job fails far more often than a 10-step one, and the [agent reliability calculator](/blog/agent-reliability-calculator/) lets you test your own. Because Construct keeps finished work in files, cutting a job into pieces that each save a file makes a rerun pick up where the last one stopped.
- **Use Activity to review runs.** Activity records what each agent did with a short reason for a limited window. It is an operating record, not a complete audit log, so have the agent append a line to a run log in Files for jobs you need to account for later.

## When might an assistant or coworker still fit?

- You only need quick answers and writing help, and you already pay for a chat assistant.
- The work has to happen inside desktop apps or local files on your own computer. Construct works in a cloud workspace and cannot operate Windows or Mac desktop apps, while Claude's scheduled tasks can use your machine ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13854387-schedule-recurring-tasks-in-claude-cowork)).
- Your company runs on Microsoft 365 and wants the agent inside Microsoft's trust boundary ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2026/06/16/copilot-cowork-is-now-generally-available/)).

For the recurring work a small team depends on, an AI employee with its own workspace, schedule, and identity is the better fit, and Construct has the lowest entry price in our table.

## How do you see past the label?

Whatever a vendor calls its product, test these:

1. **Can it start without you?** Schedule a job, close your laptop, and check the result the next morning.
2. **Where does the output go?** A thread you will not scroll back to, or a file and a record you can open next month?
3. **Whose identity does it use?** Your accounts, its own, or both, and can you choose per job?
4. **What does it remember, and can you correct it?** Inspectable memory is the difference between a colleague and a stranger every Monday.
5. **Who else can use it?** Shared workspace, roles, and channels, or one person's account?
6. **What needs approval?** Built into the product, or written into your instructions?
7. **What happens when a run fails halfway?** Does finished work survive, or does it start over?

A product that answers "no" to the first two is an assistant, whatever its homepage says. Run this test on Construct during the 7-day Pro trial, next to whatever you use now.
