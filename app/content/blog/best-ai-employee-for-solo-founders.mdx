For a one-person company, Construct is the best first AI employee. Our ranking: Construct, then Claude with Cowork, Lindy, Marblism, Manus, CellCog, Sintra, and self-hosted OpenClaw.

## Why choose Construct as your first AI employee?

- **$9 a month to start.** Lite includes up to 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Starter at $59 raises that to 5 agents and 10 scheduled tasks when you need more.
- **It keeps working while you are away.** Calendar schedules run on Construct's servers with your laptop closed, so the Monday pipeline update is waiting when you sit down.
- **Finished work becomes files.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts land in the workspace as the agent produces them. A run that stops at step 30 still keeps steps 1 to 29, and next week's run starts from last week's file.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Open what Construct has learned about your business, correct a fact once, or delete it, and later jobs use the change.
- **It grows with your first hire.** Invite a teammate as Owner, Admin, or Member. A team workspace has no member limit and no per-seat fee; usage draws on your plan.
- **It answers where you already work.** Message it from the web app, Slack, Telegram, Discord, or its own email inbox.

## How we ranked them for a one-person company

A solo founder is the buyer, the operator, and the reviewer. That changes what matters. We scored each product on five questions:

1. **What does it cost one person in a normal month?** Not the entry price, the plan you would actually need.
2. **How fast is the first useful result?** Fewer accounts to connect and fewer rules to write wins.
3. **How much checking does it need?** Anything that sends, posts, or spends on your behalf needs review, and review time is the scarcest thing a founder has.
4. **Does it keep working while you are away?** Scheduled work that runs with your laptop closed is the point of an AI employee.
5. **What happens at the limit?** A founder cannot afford a surprise bill, a silent stop, or lost work in a busy week.

## What should a solo founder budget?

This is an illustrative guide from published prices, not measured costs.

## The ranking, one by one

### 1. Construct

**Why it ranks first:** it is the cheapest paid entry on this list, and it keeps your files, schedules, and memory together in one workspace, which is what a founder's weekly jobs need.

Each Construct agent gets a persistent cloud workspace with files, a live browser, a sandbox terminal, an email address of its own, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches Gmail, Google Calendar, Notion, Linear, Jira, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable through its live integration catalog, and you can add your own MCP tools. You can message it from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord, or its own inbox: forward an email from your own address and it starts the job.

For a one-person company, three things matter most:

- **Recurring work runs without you.** Do a job once in chat, save the steps as a workflow, and put it on the Calendar. It runs on Construct's servers whether or not your laptop is open, and each result is kept in the run history. The [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) walks through it.
- **Every run builds on the last one.** A pipeline update, an investor note, or a competitor price check is saved as a dated file, so next week's run compares against last week's instead of starting over. If a run stops partway, the finished steps are already saved.
- **You stay in control of what it knows.** Construct's memory is something you can open. When a client changes its reporting format, correct the stored fact once. The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) shows how.

### 2. Claude with Cowork

**Why it ranks second:** a low-cost way to try background work if you already pay for Claude.

Cowork lets Claude run multi-step tasks on your behalf, with work running in the cloud in beta and sessions saved to your account ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-claude-cowork)). Scheduled tasks are on all paid plans and run while your computer is asleep or the Claude Desktop app is closed ([scheduled tasks](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13854387-schedule-recurring-tasks-in-claude-cowork)). You choose Manual, Auto, or Skip approval modes.

**Cost for one founder:** Pro is $20 billed monthly or $17 billed annually ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)).

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** it is your assistant rather than a separate worker. An email address of its own is not documented in the sources we reviewed, scheduled tasks cannot use folders on your own computer, and agentic work draws on the same usage as your chats, so a heavy week of background work competes with your own questions. Our [Construct vs Claude Cowork comparison](/blog/construct-vs-claude-cowork/) goes deeper.

### 3. Lindy

**Why it ranks third:** it centers on one job, your own inbox and calendar, inside Slack.

Lindy runs as an assistant in your Slack direct messages for inbox, meetings, and scheduling, and it can be texted over iMessage and SMS. Write actions can be set to require approval in Slack. Lindy's homepage offers $50 of free credits with no card ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/)). Our [Construct vs Lindy comparison](/blog/construct-vs-lindy/) goes deeper.

**Cost for one founder:** Plus is $29.99 a month with 3,000 credits, at about one cent per credit; Pro is $99.99 with 15,000 ([Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing), [plan documentation](https://docs.lindy.ai/pricing)).

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** credits do not roll over, and when they run out, "Lindy pauses credit-using actions" until the next cycle. A deep-work task costs 250 to 1,000 credits, so 3,000 credits can mean as few as three of them. Lindy's plan documentation shows computer use on Pro and above, while its pricing page lists it on every plan. The product is designed around Slack.

### 4. Marblism

**Why it ranks fourth:** it is the pick here built around answering a business phone.

Marblism sells seven AI employees, including Eva, who sorts your inbox and drafts replies without sending them ([Marblism help](https://help.marblism.com/en/articles/11516676-how-to-work-with-eva-the-exec-assistant)), and Rachel, a receptionist with her own number in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, or Mexico ([Marblism integrations](https://help.marblism.com/en/articles/15278633-what-integrations-are-available)). To use your existing business number, you set up call forwarding with your carrier ([Marblism help](https://help.marblism.com/en/articles/14827083-can-rachel-answer-calls-to-my-existing-business-number)).

**Cost for one founder:** $44 a month, or $24 a month billed yearly, for 50 hours of work across all seven employees. A drafted email uses 5 minutes, a handled call 10 minutes, and a blog post an hour ([Marblism billing](https://help.marblism.com/en/articles/15361461-marblism-pricing-plans-billing-cadence)).

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** work outside the seven roles is a poor fit, Marblism has no public API, and when the hours run out new tasks pause and the phone line pauses.

### 5. Manus

**Why it ranks fifth:** a free way to try one-off research or build tasks.

Manus 2.0, launched September 28, 2026, can start work when something happens in a connected service, such as a new email or a calendar event ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0)).

**Cost for one founder:** free with 300 daily refresh credits, 1 concurrent task, and 2 scheduled tasks; Pro from $20 a month for 4,000 credits and 20 scheduled tasks ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711111-what-is-the-current-membership-pricing-for-manus)). An always-on Cloud Computer is $30 or $50 a month extra.

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** credit use varies by task, which makes a month hard to budget, and if you stop paying for the Cloud Computer, its files and databases are deleted ([Cloud Computer billing](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/15392078-understanding-cloud-computer-plans-and-billing)). Construct's workspace files come with every plan.

### 6. CellCog

**Why it ranks sixth:** it is shaped like hiring one person into a role, priced for a bigger budget.

Each CellCog employee has its own email inbox, a task board, persistent memory, and scheduled shifts, files an end-of-day report, and can take calls on its own phone number; risky actions wait for your approval ([CellCog AI employees](https://cellcog.ai/ai-employees), [CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/)).

**Cost for one founder:** $8 a month buys 800 credits to try it, and 200 credits are free with no card. CellCog's own plan labels call the $160 tier a "Part-time AI employee, a few shifts a month" and the $500 tier "A full-time AI employee, working weekday shifts" ([CellCog pricing](https://cellcog.ai/pricing)).

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** "All payments to CellCog are final and non-refundable," and regular shift work starts at $160 a month by CellCog's own labels.

### 7. Sintra

**Why it ranks seventh:** quick marketing drafts from a set of chat helpers you direct.

Sintra sells 12 named helpers, such as Soshie for social media and Vizzy as a virtual assistant, sharing one memory called Brain AI ([Sintra](https://sintra.ai/)). Its own FAQ says "Every AI worker runs as a chatbot," and its help center says helpers "will not take actions on your behalf unless you explicitly ask them to (via chat) or set up a scheduled task/automation" ([Sintra integrations](https://help.sintra.ai/en/articles/12929400-integrations-explained)).

**Cost for one founder:** Sintra X lists at $97 a month and was on sale at $48.50 for a one-month term when we checked, with 250 credits a month ([Sintra pricing](https://sintra.ai/pricing)). A 14-day money-back guarantee applies.

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** "If a workspace runs out of credits, AI employees will stop working," and credit top-ups are non-refundable ([Sintra credits](https://help.sintra.ai/en/articles/12606676-workspace-credits)). Trustpilot shows no rating for Sintra and notes that the company "has been offering incentives for reviews" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sintra.ai)).

### 8. OpenClaw

**Why it ranks eighth:** the software is free, and you run everything yourself.

OpenClaw is MIT licensed, with no subscription and no hosted tier. It runs on macOS, Windows, or Linux, works across chat apps such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, and iMessage, and uses the models you bring ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/)).

**Cost for one founder:** the software is free; you pay for hosting and model usage.

**What it costs you compared with Construct:** installing, securing, updating, and keeping it online is your job, and it only works while your machine or server stays on. Our comparison of [building your own AI agent](/blog/construct-vs-diy/) covers the tradeoff.

## How much work can one founder supervise?

The real limit on a solo founder's AI employee is not the plan. It is your review time. A workable rule for one person is about 30 minutes of review a day. Here is how to stay inside it with Construct:

- **Ask for drafts for anything a customer sees.** Write "save a draft, do not send" into the instruction, check the draft, and send it yourself.
- **Run a job by hand three times before you schedule it.** A schedule multiplies mistakes as well as time saved.
- **Ask for outputs you can check fast.** A dated file with a source link on every line takes two minutes to verify.
- **Start with three scheduled jobs.** Lite's 3 scheduled tasks is a sensible ceiling for a first month; three good jobs beat ten you never read.

## Already using Claude, Lindy, or another agent? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to cancel anything. Move the one recurring job your current tool handles least reliably, or the one whose output you need as a file.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test longer runs and your own model keys.
2. **Copy the instructions** you gave your current agent into a Construct chat, and move the source files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every week whether or not you are online.

There is no import from other agents. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read and correct.
