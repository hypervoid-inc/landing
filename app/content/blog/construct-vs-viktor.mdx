Construct gives your team an AI employee with its own workspace, files, Calendar schedules, and email address from $9 a month, and you can hand it work from Slack, email, Telegram, Discord, or the web. Viktor starts at $50 a month for 20,000 credits shared by the workspace, and it works inside Slack and Microsoft Teams ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)).

For founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. It costs less than a fifth as much to start, it saves finished work as files your team can reopen next month instead of messages in a thread, it takes requests from more places than Slack, and it lets you read and correct what it remembers. Already using Viktor in Slack? Keep it for quick asks and give Construct the recurring jobs whose files and schedules you need to keep.

## Why choose Construct over Viktor?

- **$9 a month instead of $50.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. It is a fixed monthly price with published plan limits.
- **Work that lands in files, not only a thread.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 still leaves steps 1 to 29 behind, and next week's run opens last week's file.
- **More doors than Slack.** Message it from the web app, @mention it in Slack, text it on Telegram, use it in Discord, or email its own address.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Open what Construct has learned, correct a fact, or forget it, in the same workspace as the files and workflows that rely on it.
- **Your model, or your own keys.** Construct Pro accepts keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. Supplying your own keys is not documented in the Viktor pages we reviewed.
- **Schedules that run with your laptop closed.** A job that worked once becomes a saved workflow on the Calendar and runs server-side every week.

## What is Viktor?

Viktor describes itself as an AI employee that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams, with its own cloud computer where it writes and runs code, a catalog of more than 3,200 tools, and recurring tasks it can schedule ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

- **Slack behavior:** it responds when @mentioned in channels and to every message in DMs, and it sees a conversation only if it is a member of it ([Viktor on channels and permissions](https://viktor.com/blog/how-to-control-where-your-ai-employee-works)).
- **Approvals:** sending an email or posting on your behalf starts as a draft you approve in the thread (same source).
- **Memory:** team memory lives in the workspace, and writing or forgetting a memory asks for approval ([Viktor on memory](https://viktor.com/blog/personal-notes-vs-team-memory)).
- **Funding:** Accel led a $75 million Series A, announced on May 19, 2026 ([Accel](https://www.accel.com/news/welcome-viktor-the-ai-coworker-for-the-modern-workplace)). {/* TODO: add the Fortune report on the Series A (May 19, 2026) once its URL is confirmed; Fortune did not return it on Oct 1. */}

How Viktor's credits work, from its pricing page: credits map to what model providers charge, with no markup. Viktor's own examples put quick tasks at 100 to 300 credits, complex workflows at 500 to 1,500, and full projects at 2,000 to 5,000, so the $50 plan covers roughly 13 to 40 complex workflows a month. Unused monthly credits roll over for one month.

What this means for your budget:

- **Starting from zero:** Construct Lite costs $41 a month less than Viktor's first plan.
- **A busy month:** a Construct plan is a fixed monthly price with published plan limits and no per-message billing. On Viktor, a heavy month means watching the shared credit pool and the next tier.
- **A small team:** a Construct team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan. Starter at $59 covers up to 5 agents for the whole workspace.

## Where does Construct give you more control?

Both products give an agent a computer and a place in Slack. Construct also gives you a place where the work stays, more ways to reach it, and a say in the model and the memory behind it.

### Keep the work in a workspace, not a thread

Every Construct run saves its output to workspace files as it goes. A weekly client report becomes a folder of dated files a teammate can open next quarter without scrolling back through Slack. When several people review the same output, Construct can build a private workspace app, such as a review queue with owners and statuses; the [internal-tools guide](/blog/build-internal-tools-with-construct/) explains how.

### Reach it from more places

Message Construct from the web app, @mention it in Slack, text it on Telegram, use it in Discord, or email its own address. Mail from your own address starts a job, so you can forward a client thread with the instructions at the top. If your clients email you or your community lives on Discord, the same agent and the same files are there.

### Set roles in one team workspace

Invite teammates with Owner, Admin, or Member roles. A team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan, so adding a person never adds a seat fee. People, the agent, files, and apps share one place ([team workspaces](/use-cases/team-workspaces/)).

### Pick the model, or bring your own keys

Construct Pro can run on your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI, either as a fallback after bundled limits or for every run, as the [Construct terms](/terms/) explain. Your keys bill you directly when they are used, and you can switch providers without moving a file.

### See and fix what the agent remembers

In Construct, memory is something you can read. If a client changes its reporting format, you correct the stored fact once and every later job uses it. The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) shows how.

### Assign work in Slack

You can @mention Construct in a channel, reply in the thread that mention started, or DM it. Each install has its own access policy: allow-listed people get the agent's full tools, and if you open it to everyone, other members get a read-only tier. On plans with several agents, a channel can route to a specific agent. The [Slack briefing guide](/blog/ai-agent-slack-briefing/) shows a morning job end to end.

## Already using Viktor? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to give up Viktor. Start with the recurring job that uses the most credits, or the one whose output your team needs to find again.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test your own model keys and longer runs.
2. **Copy the instructions** you gave Viktor for the job, plus any SOPs from its team memory, into a Construct chat, and move the source files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, or add your own MCP tools.
4. **Install Construct in Slack, @mention it with the job, and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every week whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Viktor's credits, channel context, deployed apps, and memory do not transfer. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read. Three things to set up deliberately. Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so for anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them. Construct does not read Slack channel history, so put the messages a job needs in a file or paste them in. And scheduled runs report in the web app with a notification rather than posting into Slack.

## When might Viktor still fit?

- Your company runs on Microsoft Teams.
- You want an agent that reads the Slack channels it sits in and drafts every outgoing action for approval in the thread.
- You need SOC 2 paperwork today: Viktor lists SOC 2 Type 1, with Type 2 and ISO 27001 in progress ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

For recurring team work that needs files, schedules, a choice of model, and a price a small business can justify, Construct is the better fit.
