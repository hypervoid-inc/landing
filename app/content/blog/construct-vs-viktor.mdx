The best Viktor AI alternative for a small team is Construct: an AI employee with its own workspace, files, Calendar schedules, and email address from $9 a month, that you can hand work from Slack, email, Telegram, Discord, or the web. Viktor starts at $50 a month for 20,000 credits shared by the workspace, and it works inside Slack and Microsoft Teams ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)).

For founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. It costs less than a fifth as much to start, it saves finished work as files your team can reopen next month instead of messages in a thread, it takes requests from more places than Slack, and it lets you read and correct what it remembers. Already using Viktor in Slack? Keep it for quick asks and give Construct the recurring jobs whose files and schedules you need to keep.

## Why choose Construct over Viktor?

Construct costs $41 a month less to start, keeps every result as a file, and reaches your team in five places instead of two. The details:

- **$9 a month instead of $50.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. It is a fixed monthly price with published plan limits.
- **Work that lands in files, not only a thread.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 still leaves steps 1 to 29 behind, and next week's run opens last week's file.
- **More doors than Slack.** Message it from the web app, @mention it in Slack, text it on Telegram, use it in Discord, or email its own address.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Open what Construct has learned, correct a fact, or forget it, in the same workspace as the files and workflows that rely on it.
- **Your model, or your own keys.** Construct Pro accepts keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. Supplying your own keys is not documented in the Viktor pages we reviewed.
- **Roles for the whole team today.** Construct team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles. Viktor lists role-based access controls as a roadmap priority ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

## What is Viktor AI?

Viktor is an AI employee that "lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams," with its own cloud computer where it writes and runs code, connections to more than 3,200 tools, and recurring tasks it can schedule ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

- **Slack behavior:** it responds when @mentioned in channels and to every message in one-to-one DMs, and "Viktor sees a conversation only if he is a member of it" ([Viktor on channels and permissions](https://viktor.com/blog/how-to-control-where-your-ai-employee-works)).
- **Approvals:** "sensitive actions like sending an email or posting on your behalf start as drafts" that you approve in the thread (same source).
- **Memory:** team memory is shared across the workspace, and in your personal memory store "anything that writes or forgets a memory asks for your approval first" ([Viktor on memory](https://viktor.com/blog/personal-notes-vs-team-memory)).
- **Access:** a connection can be scoped to "a specific Google Drive folder, a single HubSpot pipeline, or one GitHub repository" ([Viktor on access control](https://viktor.com/blog/how-to-control-what-your-ai-employee-can-access)).
- **Funding:** Accel led a $75 million Series A, announced on May 19, 2026 ([Accel](https://www.accel.com/news/welcome-viktor-the-ai-coworker-for-the-modern-workplace); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/19/viktor-ai-startup-raises-75-million-for-virtual-coworker-exclusive/)).

How Viktor's credits work, from its pricing page: credits cover model costs with "no markup," and there is no per-seat charge. Viktor's own examples put quick tasks at 100 to 300 credits, complex workflows at 500 to 1,500, and full projects at 2,000 to 5,000, so the $50 plan covers roughly 13 to 40 complex workflows a month. Viktor's own estimate for 20,000 credits is "roughly 40-200 tasks depending on complexity." Unused monthly plan credits roll over for one month.

What this means for your budget:

- **Starting from zero:** Construct Lite costs $41 a month less than Viktor's first plan.
- **A busy month:** a Construct plan is a fixed monthly price with published plan limits and no per-message billing. On Viktor, a heavy month means watching the shared credit pool and the next tier.
- **A small team:** a Construct team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan. Starter at $59 covers up to 5 agents for the whole workspace.

## Where does Construct give you more control?

Both products give an agent a computer and a place in Slack. Construct also gives you a place where the work stays, more ways to reach it, and a say in the model and the memory behind it.

### Keep the work in a workspace, not a thread

Every Construct run saves its output to workspace files as it goes. A weekly client report becomes a folder of dated files a teammate can open next quarter without scrolling back through Slack. When several people review the same output, Construct can build a private workspace app, such as a review queue with owners and statuses; the [internal-tools guide](/blog/build-internal-tools-with-construct/) explains how.

### Reach it from more places

Message Construct from the web app, @mention it in Slack, text it on Telegram, use it in Discord, or email its own address. Mail from your own address starts a job, so you can forward a client thread with the instructions at the top. If your clients email you or your community lives on Discord, the same agent and the same files are there.

### Set roles in one team workspace

Invite teammates with Owner, Admin, or Member roles. A team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan, so adding a person never adds a seat fee. People, the agent, files, and apps share one place ([team workspaces](/use-cases/team-workspaces/)).

### Pick the model, or bring your own keys

Construct Pro can run on your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI, either as a fallback after bundled limits or for every run, as the [Construct terms](/terms/) explain. Your keys bill you directly when they are used, and you can switch providers without moving a file.

### See and fix what the agent remembers

In Construct, memory is something you can read. If a client changes its reporting format, you correct the stored fact once and every later job uses it. The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) shows how.

## Can Construct work in Slack like Viktor?

Yes, for assigning work: you can @mention Construct in a channel, reply in the thread that mention started, or DM it. Each install has its own access policy: allow-listed people get the agent's full tools, and if you open it to everyone, other members get a read-only tier. On plans with several agents, a channel can route to a specific agent.

Two differences to plan around. Construct acts on the messages addressed to it and does not read a channel's history, so put the messages a job needs in a file, paste them in, or point it at the system the channel talks about, such as HubSpot. And scheduled runs report in the web app with a notification rather than posting into Slack; the [Slack briefing guide](/blog/ai-agent-slack-briefing/) shows how a morning job still reaches a channel through its email address.

## How to test Viktor and Construct on the same job

We have not run a side-by-side test, so here is a checklist for running one yourself in under an hour. Pick one recurring Slack job, such as a weekly summary of open deals, and give both products the identical prompt, ending with "draft only, send nothing."

1. **Seed the input.** Put a week of deal notes in a shared file, so both agents read the same facts.
2. **Time the setup.** Note the minutes from sign-up to the first usable result on each.
3. **Check the result line by line.** Count missed or wrong items against your notes.
4. **Find the output a day later.** Can a teammate who was not in the thread open it?
5. **Run it again next week.** Does the second run compare against the first?
6. **Record the cost.** Credits used on Viktor's usage page; share of plan usage on Construct.
7. **Note anything sent or posted** that you did not ask for.

On Construct, save the steps as a workflow and put it on the Calendar once the first file is right; the [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) walks through each step.

## Already using Viktor? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to give up Viktor. Start with the recurring job that uses the most credits, or the one whose output your team needs to find again.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test your own model keys and longer runs.
2. **Copy the instructions** you gave Viktor for the job, plus any rules from its team memory, into a Construct chat, and move the source files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, or add your own MCP tools.
4. **Install Construct in Slack, @mention it with the job, and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the [Calendar](/use-cases/scheduling/)** so it runs every week whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Viktor's credits, channel context, artifacts, and memory do not transfer. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read. One thing to set up deliberately: Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so for anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them.

## When might Viktor still fit?

- Your company runs on Microsoft Teams.
- You want an agent that reads the Slack channels it sits in and drafts every outgoing action for approval in the thread.
- You need SOC 2 paperwork today: Viktor's FAQ lists SOC 2 Type 1, with Type 2 and ISO 27001 in progress ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

For recurring team work that needs files, schedules, a choice of model, and a price a small business can justify, Construct is the better fit.
