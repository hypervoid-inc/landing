## Why choose Construct over Viktor?

- **$9 a month instead of $50.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Starter at $59 covers up to 5 agents and 10 scheduled tasks.
- **Work that lands in a workspace, not a thread.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29, and next week's job starts from last week's file.
- **More than Slack.** The same agent answers in Slack, Telegram, Discord, its own email inbox, and the web, so client email and a Discord community reach it too.
- **Schedules you can see.** Recurring jobs sit on a Calendar and run server-side at the time you set, with every laptop closed.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Inspect what the agent has learned, correct a fact, or tell it to forget one.
- **A team without seat counting.** Team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles and no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan.

## What is Viktor, and what does it cost?

Viktor is an AI employee for Slack and Microsoft Teams that connects to more than 3,200 tools, runs code on its own cloud computer, schedules recurring tasks, and asks for confirmation before high-stakes actions ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)). In channels it works when @mentioned, in direct messages it answers every message, and access follows channel membership ([Viktor on channels and permissions](https://viktor.com/blog/how-to-control-where-your-ai-employee-works)).

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Team plans start at $50 a month for 20,000 credits, then $75 for 30,000, $100 for 40,000, and $200 for 80,000. Billing is per workspace, and the team shares the same credits. Unused monthly plan credits roll over for one month, and new workspaces get $100 in credits with no card ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)). Viktor's own examples put quick tasks at 100 to 300 credits, complex workflows at 500 to 1,500, and full projects at 2,000 to 5,000.

## Why do teams look for a Viktor alternative?

Viktor's co-founder described the tradeoff of building only for Slack on Hacker News: "You inherit every Slack UX limitation. You can't build custom UI components. Your entire interaction model is text, threads, buttons, and emoji" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47266158)). That explains most of the reasons teams compare alternatives:

- **Credits in a busy month.** At $50 for 20,000 credits, Viktor's own ranges work out to roughly 66 to 200 quick tasks, or 13 to 40 complex workflows, before you need a bigger plan.
- **Where the work lives.** Teams that want a folder of dated reports, a schedule they can inspect, and memory they can correct want a workspace behind the chat.
- **Channels outside Slack and Teams.** If clients email you, or your community lives in Discord or Telegram, a coworker that lives in Slack misses part of the work.
- **The subscription you already have.** If your company pays for Claude Team, ChatGPT Pro, or Perplexity, part of the need may already be in your bill.

## What does a Slack AI employee cost for a six-person team?

This is an illustrative calculation from list prices at monthly billing, not a measured test. Usage allowances differ, so a lower total does not mean equal work.

Imagine a six-person agency. Everyone wants to ask the agent for client research in Slack, and one recurring job should produce a Monday summary of open deals.

- **Construct:** Lite at $9 covers 2 agents and 3 scheduled tasks for a whole team workspace, with no member limit; Starter at $59 is the step up when several people lean on it daily, with 6x Lite usage and 10 scheduled tasks.
- **Viktor:** $50 for the workspace and 20,000 shared credits, about 13 to 40 complex workflows a month at Viktor's published ranges before the $75 or $100 plan.
- **Runbear Team:** $99 for the workspace and 20,000 credits, at 30 to 50 or more credits for a multi-step tool task.
- **Lindy Plus:** $29.99 per user, so about $180 for six people.
- **Claude Tag:** Team seats for the people who need Claude elsewhere, plus channel usage from a balance you fund. The total depends on a pilot.

The deciding questions are whether the whole team shares one plan, what happens in a busy month, and whether you can find each job's output afterward. Construct answers the last one with a file in the workspace every time.

## If you want the work to land in a workspace

### 1. Construct

**Best for:** founders and small teams whose recurring work crosses several apps, should leave files behind, and arrives in more places than Slack.

Each agent works from a persistent cloud workspace with files, live browser runs, a sandbox terminal, a native agent email address, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, Airtable, and more through a live integration catalog, and you can add custom MCP tools when a connection does not exist.

**How it works in Slack:** @mention Construct in a channel, reply in the thread it starts, or send it a direct message. Each Slack install has an access policy. By default only the person who installed it is admitted. People you add to the allow list get the agent's full tools, and if you open it to everyone, other members get a read-only tier that can search the web, read workspace context, and look up memory, but cannot touch files, email, integrations, or the browser. A deny list mutes anyone. On plans with several agents, you can route a channel to a specific agent. Work started in Slack lands in the workspace, so the file, the memory, and the run history are there when someone opens the web desktop or messages the same agent from Telegram.

What a Viktor user gets with it:

- **Files instead of thread history.** Every finished report, CSV, or script is a workspace file, written as it is produced, that teammates can open from the web desktop.
- **A Calendar of recurring jobs.** Save a successful run as a workflow and schedule it: 3 on Lite, 10 on Starter, 50 on Pro. Schedules run server-side.
- **Memory you control.** Tell it once that the sales team counts a deal as open until a contract is signed, and correct that fact later if the rule changes.

And scheduled runs report in the web app with a notification rather than posting into Slack, so the Monday summary waits as a file for whoever opens it. Workflows also run straight through without an approval step, so for anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself.

## AI teammates built for Slack

### 2. Runbear

**Best for:** a team that wants several named agents in Slack, each with its own knowledge, such as @Support or @SalesOps.

Runbear runs shared agents in Slack and Microsoft Teams with per-user auth and connected tools ([Runbear](https://runbear.io/)). Its docs describe a team agent that acts on its own, a personal Inbox Agent that prepares drafts only you see, and triggers on a schedule, a webhook, or an app event ([Runbear docs](https://docs.runbear.io/)).

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Team is $99 a month per workspace for 20,000 credits and 5 builders across Slack, Discord, and Microsoft Teams. Business is $399 for 100,000 credits and 10 builders, with bring-your-own LLM key, SAML SSO, and an audit log ([Runbear pricing](https://runbear.io/pricing)). New organizations get a 14-day Team trial ([Runbear billing docs](https://docs.runbear.io/administration/billing-and-credits)). A knowledge-base answer costs 10 to 20 credits and a multi-step tool task 30 to 50 or more, and "Agents pause when the organization reaches its credit limit." A cloud computer, browser, or terminal is not described in the pages we reviewed.

### 3. Lindy

**Best for:** teams where each person wants a private assistant for their own inbox and meetings.

In channels the team shares one Lindy summoned with an @mention, and in DMs each person gets a private Lindy for inbox, meetings, and scheduling ([Lindy quickstart](https://docs.lindy.ai/start-here/quickstart)). Routines run on a schedule or on an event, with a dry run on real data ([Lindy Routines](https://docs.lindy.ai/teammate/routines)).

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Plus is $29.99 per user a month with 3,000 credits, Pro $99.99 with 15,000, and Max $199.99 with 35,000 ([Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing)). Billing is per user, so six people on Plus pay about $180 a month. The pricing page lists computer use on every plan, while the [plan documentation](https://docs.lindy.ai/pricing) shows it from Pro upward. Our [Construct vs Lindy comparison](/blog/construct-vs-lindy/) and [Lindy alternatives](/blog/lindy-alternatives/) go further.

### 4. Claude Tag

**Best for:** teams already on Claude Team or Enterprise.

Claude Tag puts Claude in Slack channels with admin-governed access and is labelled Public Beta. Work runs in an ephemeral sandbox that is "discarded when the conversation goes idle," channels keep their own notes as memory, and the docs list scheduled jobs, channel watching, and triggers ([Claude Tag overview](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/overview)). Files do not persist between conversations unless they go to GitHub, a doc, or a ticket.

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Team and Enterprise plans only. "Adding Claude to Slack doesn't add a per-seat charge"; channel work draws from a usage balance the organization funds under a monthly spend limit ([Claude Tag overview](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/overview)). A Team standard seat is $25 a month ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). An Owner pairs the Slack workspace and sets the limit ([Claude Tag setup](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/admins/setup-overview)).

## If your company already pays for an AI subscription

### 5. ChatGPT Dots

**Best for:** people already on ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium.

OpenAI launched Dots on September 29, 2026. A dot can be messaged or called in ChatGPT and messaged in Slack and Teams, has its own cloud computer and browser, and starts with built-in approval rules ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday)). The first dot is included with Pro and Business Premium; reports disagree on whether the $100 Pro tier counts, so budget $200 a month for one person, as our [ChatGPT Dots alternatives guide](/blog/chatgpt-dots-alternatives/) explains. A dot belongs to one person's ChatGPT account, and personal Pro subscribers in the EEA, Switzerland, and the UK were excluded at launch. See [Construct vs ChatGPT Dots](/blog/construct-vs-openai-dots/).

### 6. Manus

**Best for:** one-off research and build tasks started from a Slack channel.

An @Manus mention in a channel creates a task the whole team can see, a DM is private, and results come back as PDFs, media, or preview links ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/14431752-chatting-with-manus-agent-on-slack-channels-dms-and-file-delivery)). Manus 2.0, announced September 28, 2026, added event-triggered Automations and an optional Cloud Computer ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0)).

**Price, checked October 1, 2026:** Free has 300 daily credits, 1 concurrent task, and 2 scheduled tasks. Pro starts at $20 a month for 4,000 credits with 20 scheduled tasks, and Team at $20 per seat ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711111-what-is-the-current-membership-pricing-for-manus)). The Cloud Computer is $30 or $50 a month extra ([Cloud Computer billing](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/15392078-understanding-cloud-computer-plans-and-billing)). Monthly credits do not roll over, and new tasks cannot start at zero credits ([Manus credit rules](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711097-what-are-the-rules-for-credits-consumption-and-how-can-i-obtain-them)).

### 7. Perplexity Computer

**Best for:** research-heavy requests from Slack, such as a market brief.

Perplexity's Slack app answers DMs and `@Computer` mentions in threads with citations and needs a Perplexity plan with Computer and a paid Slack plan ([Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/14016915-using-perplexity-in-slack)). Computer runs on credits at "100 credits = $1"; Pro has a one-time 4,000-credit bonus and Max includes 10,000 credits a month ([How credits work](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13838041-how-credits-work-on-perplexity)), with Pro at $17 and Max at $167 a month billed annually ([Perplexity Pro](https://www.perplexity.ai/pro)). ## Already using Viktor? Add Construct in 15 minutes

Keep Viktor in the channels where it already works, and give Construct the recurring job whose output your team keeps hunting for in old threads.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test longer runs and your own model keys.
2. **Add Construct to Slack** from Settings, then Messaging, add the people who need full access to the allow list, and invite it to the channel.
3. **Copy the instructions** you gave Viktor into an @mention, and move the reference files into the workspace.
4. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, run it once, and check the result in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every week whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Viktor's history, workflows, and learned context do not transfer, so write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read.

- You need a confirmation step before high-stakes actions built into every job.
