Viktor starts at $50 a month per workspace for 20,000 credits, works in Slack and Microsoft Teams, and returns its results in a Slack thread, as uploaded files, or as apps it deploys.

For founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. The other six options below suit narrower cases: Runbear for several named agents per team, Lindy for per-person inbox help, Claude Tag if you already pay for Claude Team, and Dots, Manus, or Perplexity Computer if your company already has one of those subscriptions.

## Why choose Construct over Viktor?

Construct costs $9 a month instead of $50, saves its work as files rather than thread replies, and reaches your team beyond Slack. In detail:

- **$9 a month instead of $50.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Starter at $59 covers up to 5 agents and 10 scheduled tasks.
- **Work that lands in a workspace, not a thread.** Reports, spreadsheets, and scripts are saved to workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29, and next week's job starts from last week's file.
- **More than Slack.** The same agent answers in Slack, Telegram, Discord, its own email inbox, and the web, so client email and a Discord community reach it too.
- **Schedules you can see.** Recurring jobs sit on a Calendar and run server-side at the time you set, with every laptop closed.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Inspect what the agent has learned, correct a fact, or tell it to forget one.
- **A team without seat counting.** Team workspaces have Owner, Admin, and Member roles and no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan.

## What is Viktor AI, and how much does it cost?

Viktor is an "AI employee that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams," and its Team plans start at $50 a month for 20,000 credits, billed per workspace rather than per seat ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)). It connects to more than 3,200 tools, has its "own computer in the cloud where he writes and runs code," schedules recurring tasks, and asks for confirmation before high-stakes actions such as sending email or deploying to production ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

In channels Viktor works when @mentioned, in a one-to-one DM it answers every message, and it reads only the conversations it is a member of, so access follows channel membership ([Viktor on channels and permissions](https://viktor.com/blog/how-to-control-where-your-ai-employee-works)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** $50 a month for 20,000 credits, $75 for 30,000, $100 for 40,000, and $200 for 80,000, with larger tiers up to $50,000 a month and custom Enterprise pricing. The whole team shares the same credits. Unused monthly plan credits now roll over for one month, and new workspaces get $100 in credits with no card. Viktor's own examples put quick tasks at 100 to 300 credits, complex workflows at 500 to 1,500, and full projects at 2,000 to 5,000, and its FAQ estimates that 20,000 credits cover roughly 40 to 200 tasks ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)). Annual pricing is not documented in the sources we reviewed.

Accel led a $75 million Series A in Viktor, announced on May 19, 2026 ([Accel](https://www.accel.com/news/welcome-viktor-the-ai-coworker-for-the-modern-workplace)).

## Why do teams look for a Viktor alternative?

Most teams compare alternatives because of credits in a busy month, because they want the output somewhere other than a thread, or because some of their work arrives outside Slack. Viktor's co-founder described the tradeoff of building only for Slack on Hacker News: "You inherit every Slack UX limitation. You can't build custom UI components. Your entire interaction model is text, threads, buttons, and emoji" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47266158)).

- **Credits in a busy month.** At $50 for 20,000 credits, Viktor's own ranges work out to roughly 66 to 200 quick tasks, or 13 to 40 complex workflows, before you need a bigger plan.
- **Where the work lives.** Teams that want a folder of dated reports, a schedule they can inspect, and memory they can correct want a workspace behind the chat.
- **Channels outside Slack and Teams.** If clients email you, or your community lives in Discord or Telegram, a coworker that lives in Slack misses part of the work.
- **The subscription you already have.** If your company pays for Claude Team, ChatGPT Pro, or Perplexity, part of the need may already be in your bill.

## If you want the work to land in a workspace

### 1. Construct

**Best for:** founders and small teams whose recurring work crosses several apps, should leave files behind, and arrives in more places than Slack.

Each agent works from a persistent cloud workspace with files, live browser runs, a sandbox terminal, a native agent email address, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, Airtable, and more through a live integration catalog, and you can add custom MCP tools when a connection does not exist.

**How it works in Slack:** @mention Construct in a channel, reply in the thread it starts, or send it a direct message. Each Slack install has an access policy. The default is **Only people I allow**, so only the person who installed it is admitted. People you add to the **Full access** list get the agent's full tools. If you switch to **Everyone in the server**, other members get a read-only tier that can search the web, fetch pages, and read workspace context, but has no memory and cannot touch files, email, connected apps, or the browser. A blocked list mutes anyone, the installer included. On plans with several agents, you can route a channel to a specific agent. Teammates who want private DMs link their own Construct account first; Slack shows them a link card. Work started in Slack lands in the workspace, so the file, the memory, and the run history are there when someone opens the web desktop or messages the same agent from Telegram.

## AI teammates built for Slack

### 2. Runbear

**Best for:** a team that wants several named agents in Slack, each with its own knowledge, such as @Support or @SalesOps.

Runbear runs team agents in Slack, Microsoft Teams, and Discord that read a message, run tools, search knowledge, and post a reply, plus a personal Inbox Agent that watches your Slack mentions, Gmail, and Google Calendar and prepares drafts only you see. Agents can also run on a schedule, a webhook, or an event in a connected app ([Runbear docs](https://docs.runbear.io/)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** Team is $99 a month for 20,000 credits and 5 builders across Slack, Discord, and Microsoft Teams. Business is $399 for 100,000 credits and 10 builders, with bring-your-own LLM key, SAML SSO, and an audit log. Runbear "charges by usage, not by seat" ([Runbear pricing](https://runbear.io/pricing)). New organizations can start a 14-day Team trial when Runbear is offering trials. A knowledge-base answer typically costs 10 to 20 credits and a multi-step tool task 30 to 50 or more, unused credits expire at the end of each billing period, and "Agents pause when the organization reaches its credit limit" unless an admin allows overage ([Runbear billing docs](https://docs.runbear.io/administration/billing-and-credits)). A cloud computer, browser, or terminal is not documented in the sources we reviewed; tools are described as "Live actions and lookups through MCP servers."

### 3. Lindy

**Best for:** teams where each person wants a private assistant for their own inbox and meetings.

In Slack channels the team shares one Lindy summoned with an @mention, and in DMs each person gets a private Lindy for inbox, meetings, and scheduling. You can also text it by SMS or iMessage ([Lindy quickstart](https://docs.lindy.ai/start-here/quickstart)). Routines run on a schedule or on an event ([Lindy Routines](https://docs.lindy.ai/teammate/routines)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** Lindy now has three plans: Free, with $50 in credits that last 7 days and no card, Team, billed per active user, and custom Enterprise. Team starts at $29.99 per active user a month for 3,000 credits (Lindy's pricing page also shows $49.99 for 5,000 credits to some visitors), credits pool across the team, and top-ups are $10 per 1,000 credits ([Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing)). Anyone who uses Lindy counts as an active user, including someone who only @mentions it in Slack, so six people on Team pay $179.94 a month at the lower price. The [plan documentation](https://docs.lindy.ai/pricing) points to in-app billing for current prices. Our [Construct vs Lindy comparison](/blog/construct-vs-lindy/) and [Lindy alternatives](/blog/lindy-alternatives/) go further.

### 4. Claude Tag

**Best for:** teams already on Claude Team or Enterprise.

Claude Tag puts Claude in Slack channels with admin-governed access and is labelled Public Beta. Anyone in a channel it has joined can tag it, and it posts a checklist in the thread as it works. Each task runs in an ephemeral sandbox that is "discarded when the conversation goes idle," so work that should outlast the conversation has to be saved somewhere else, such as a pull request, doc, or ticket. The docs also cover channel memory and scheduled jobs, channel watching, and triggers ([Claude Tag overview](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/overview)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** Team and Enterprise plans only. "Adding Claude to Slack doesn't add a per-seat charge"; channel work draws from a usage balance the organization funds, under a spend limit an Owner sets ([Claude Tag overview](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/overview)). On a Team plan nothing runs until usage credits are loaded ([Claude Tag setup](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/admins/setup-overview)). A Team standard seat is $25 a month billed monthly, or $20 billed annually ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)).

## If your company already pays for an AI subscription

**Best for:** people already on ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium.

OpenAI launched dots on September 29, 2026. A dot can be messaged or called in ChatGPT and messaged in Slack and Teams, has its own cloud computer and browser, and starts with built-in rules on when it needs your sign-off ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday)). The first dot is included with Pro and Business Premium; reports disagree on whether the $100 Pro tier counts, so budget $200 a month for one person, as our [ChatGPT Dots alternatives guide](/blog/chatgpt-dots-alternatives/) explains. ### 6. Manus

**Best for:** one-off research and build tasks started from a Slack channel.

An @Manus mention in a channel creates a task the whole team can see, a DM is private, and files come back in the chat, with presentations converted to PDF and web projects sent as preview links ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/14431752-chatting-with-manus-agent-on-slack-channels-dms-and-file-delivery)). Manus 2.0, announced September 28, 2026, added event-triggered Automations and an optional Cloud Computer ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** Free has 300 daily credits, 1 concurrent task, and 2 scheduled automations. Pro starts at $20 a month for 4,000 credits with 20 scheduled automations, and Team at $20 per seat ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711111-what-is-the-current-membership-pricing-for-manus)). The Cloud Computer is $30 or $50 a month extra ([Cloud Computer billing](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/15392078-understanding-cloud-computer-plans-and-billing)). Monthly credits do not roll over, and new tasks cannot start once available credits reach zero ([Manus credit rules](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711097-what-are-the-rules-for-credits-consumption-and-how-can-i-obtain-them)).

### 7. Perplexity Computer

**Best for:** research-heavy requests from Slack, such as a market brief.

Perplexity's Slack app, branded Computer, answers DMs and `@Computer` mentions in threads, can search the web and the Slack messages the asking user can already see, and needs a Perplexity plan that includes Computer and a paid Slack plan. Usage counts against each user's own Computer limits ([Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/14016915-using-perplexity-in-slack)).

**Price, checked October 11, 2026:** Pro is $20 a month ($17 billed annually) and includes weekly Computer usage plus a one-time 4,000-credit bonus that expires in 30 days. Max is $200 a month ($167 billed annually) and starts with 10,000 credits a month ([How credits work](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13838041-how-credits-work-on-perplexity), [Perplexity pricing](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/pricing)). ## Is Slackbot a Viktor alternative?

Slackbot is Slack's own AI agent, included with Slack's Business+ plan under a weekly message limit and with unlimited access on Enterprise+; Free and Pro workspaces get only a limited trial ([Slackbot](https://slack.com/features/slackbot)). It can take actions in connected tools through MCP and build repeatable workflows you ask for. Tagging Slackbot into a channel so the whole team works on one answer was in early pilot as of Slack's September 15, 2026 Dreamforce announcement ([Slack](https://slack.com/blog/news/ai-powered-interface-for-work)).

That makes it a personal assistant for each Business+ user more than a shared coworker today, which is why it is not ranked above. If you already pay for Business+, use Slackbot for personal questions and give Construct the shared, recurring work that should leave a file behind.

## How do you test a Viktor alternative yourself?

Run the same small job on two products for two weeks and compare what is left behind, not how the first reply reads. A checklist you can use:

1. **Pick one real weekly request** your team already makes in Slack, such as "summarize open deals from HubSpot with owner and next step, draft only, send nothing."
2. **Give both agents identical instructions and sources.** Note the setup minutes for each.
3. **Record cost per run:** credits or usage consumed, and what that is in dollars on your plan.
4. **Check the output against the source** and count misses and anything invented.
5. **Look for the result a week later.** Can a teammate who was not in the thread find it?
6. **Confirm nothing was sent or changed** without your say-so, and note where you would read that record.
7. **Run week two on a schedule** and check it builds on week one instead of starting over.

## Already using Viktor? Add Construct in 15 minutes

Keep Viktor in the channels where it already works, and give Construct the recurring job whose output your team keeps hunting for in old threads.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test longer runs and your own model keys.
2. **Add Construct to Slack** from Settings, then Messaging, and add the people who need the agent's full tools to the **Full access** list.
3. **@mention it in the channel** with the instructions you gave Viktor (invite it first if the channel is private), and move the reference files into the workspace.
4. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog, run it once, and check the result in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every week whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Viktor's history, workflows, and learned context do not transfer, so write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read.
