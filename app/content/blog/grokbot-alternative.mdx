Lite does not include schedules or native agent email; those start on Starter at $59. Bring-your-own-key requires Pro at $299.

## What is Grok Bot?

Grok Bot is the agent product from the company behind Grok. You assign work to bots that use a cloud computer, sign into tools, and coordinate across tasks. It is different from asking a question in Grok chat: the product is designed to act inside the applications where work happens.

Its strengths include parallel teammates, shared context, and learning a routine by watching you perform it. The [Grok Bot introduction](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) describes these capabilities and access through eligible Grok and Cursor subscriptions.

That makes it a relevant comparison for Construct. Both belong in the conversation about an [AI employee for real business work](/blog/ai-employee/). An alternative has to offer a useful operating environment, not merely a different chat box.

## Methodology

We build Construct. This is our editorial comparison of documented capabilities and published prices, checked on **September 10, 2026**. It is not a hands-on performance benchmark. Plan limits matter throughout; where an equivalent capability is not documented in the Grok Bot sources we reviewed, we say so rather than treating missing documentation as proof of absence.

Sources: [Construct pricing](/pricing/) and [Grok Bot pricing](https://x.ai/bot). Subscriptions and limits can change; check both before purchasing.

Construct Lite's starting subscription is $11/month below Cursor Pro and $21/month below SuperGrok. That gives a new user a cheaper way to try assigning work to an AI employee. It does **not** establish equal usage, equal features, or a lower cost per completed task.

If you already pay for an eligible Grok or Cursor subscription, Bot access is included. Trying that allowance first may cost you no additional subscription fee. And if scheduling is your requirement, compare Grok Bot's included routines against Construct Starter, not Lite.

Older launch comparisons may show a higher entry price. Grok Bot's [August 26 access expansion](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans) explicitly added SuperGrok and Cursor Pro, so those older price gates are not the basis of this comparison.

For Construct, start by deciding whether you need occasional tasks, recurring operations, or your own model credentials. The subscription has usage limits, and Pro's BYOK adds provider charges when your keys are used. Buying the smallest suitable plan is more useful than buying the largest headline allowance.

## Where Construct gives you more control

Extensibility becomes concrete the first time an agent needs a tool outside the standard setup. Can you connect your internal system? Can the team review its output in an app? Can you choose how model access is supplied? Construct gives you documented paths for each.

### Connect the tools your business actually uses

Construct discovers supported applications through its live integration catalog. That includes services such as Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable. You can also add custom MCP tools, allowing the agent to reach a system through an interface your team provides.

For example, an agency might keep client approvals in a small internal service. A custom tool could expose the current approval status, giving Construct a direct way to retrieve it alongside the report files. Your team supplies and maintains that connection; you do not have to wait for a new catalog entry.

Grok Bot has its own [public marketplace](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace), including bots for specialist jobs and plugin-related work. It would be misleading to call it uncustomizable. Construct's appeal here is the combination of supported connections, custom tools, and workspace apps in one operating environment.

### Give repeated work a small internal app

A report in chat works well until several people need to review it. Then you want an owner, a status, a filter, and a place to mark it ready.

Construct can build a private workspace app for that process. The source remains in Files, successful builds open in the desktop, and a failed edit leaves the last successful build available. Our [internal-tools guide](/blog/build-internal-tools-with-construct/) covers how that works.

Grok Bot also has inline cards and widgets, as its [design article](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot) shows. Construct's specific advantage for this use case is its documented private-app build and maintenance lifecycle, including the last working build.

These are constrained private apps: they declare permissions and do not support arbitrary package installation. The finished app also does not inherit every tool available to the agent that built it.

### Bring your own model keys when you need them

Construct Pro supports credentials for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. BYOK can be off, used as an automatic fallback after bundled limits, or configured for exclusive use. The [Construct terms](/terms/) explain the supported providers and billing responsibility.

That gives teams with existing provider accounts a way to use them in Construct. It also means choosing Construct does not require giving up xAI as a provider. This is a specific model-access option, not a promise that every provider exposes identical models or behavior.

Here the product difference is explicit: Grok Bot's [settings documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications) says Cursor manages model selection and there is no model picker. Construct Pro's BYOK is a useful reason to choose it when supplying your own model access matters.

### Correct the context behind the work

An agent remembering an old preference can create extra work every week. Construct lets you inspect stored memory, correct a fact, or forget it. If a client changes its reporting format, you can update the context the next job relies on.

The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) explains that control. Activity summaries and bounded chat tool records also help you investigate a run. They are useful operating records, not a complete immutable audit log. For a team reviewing delegated work, knowing those boundaries is part of choosing the product.

## A practical example: the weekly client report

Imagine a small agency preparing a Friday account update. This is an illustrative workflow, not a customer case study or a measured comparison.

In Construct, start with the client list and reporting instructions saved in the workspace. Connect the relevant service, then ask the agent to read this week's records, compare them with the previous report, and save a draft with links to its sources. Open the file and check the numbers before adding any delivery step.

After a few successful runs, save the process as a workflow and schedule it on Starter or above. Keep dated reports so next week's job has a reference. If several people review the output, add a small workspace app to track who has checked each report.

Current workflows are linear; do not assume a visual branching engine, approval gates, or automatic recovery. Keep review explicit in the instructions and leave customer delivery out of the scheduled job until you want it included. The [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) explains the current boundaries.

Evaluate the result by asking whether the report is useful, its sources are traceable, and the process can be run again with less setup. Those are better purchasing criteria than how impressive the first conversation feels.

## Which should you choose?

Choose Construct when you want a low-cost entry into an AI workspace, then room to add connected tools, private apps, and provider keys as your needs grow. Its strongest case is a small team's recurring work: the files, context, interface, and next run all have somewhere to live.

Grok Bot deserves a look if you already have eligible access or prefer teaching routines by demonstration and coordinating several bots through conversation. Those are real strengths described in its [launch documentation](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot).

If you are moving work to Construct, start with one procedure. Bring its instructions and source files, reconnect the needed accounts, and test the result before recreating the schedule. Do not assume a one-click import of bots, credentials, or memories.

## Give Construct one real job

Pick the report, research task, or small tracker that keeps slipping into your evening. Give Construct the inputs and describe what a finished result should contain. Start with Lite for a small task, or use the seven-day Pro trial to evaluate broader capabilities.
