Construct gives you an AI employee with its own workspace, files, schedules, and email address from $9 a month, with no other subscription needed first. Grok Bot comes bundled with a Cursor Pro ($20) or SuperGrok ($30) subscription, and its settings documentation says Cursor manages model selection, with no model picker.

For founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. It costs less to start, lets you bring your own model keys, keeps memory you can read and correct, and builds small private apps so your team can review repeated work in one place. Already have Grok Bot through Cursor or SuperGrok? Keep it, and give Construct the recurring jobs your team needs to see.

## Why choose Construct over Grok Bot?

- **$9 a month, on its own.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address. Grok Bot access arrives with a $20 Cursor Pro or $30 SuperGrok subscription.
- **Your own model keys.** Construct Pro runs on your keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI. Grok Bot's [settings documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications) says Cursor manages model selection and there is no model picker.
- **Memory you can read and fix.** Open what Construct has learned, correct a fact, or forget it, so a stale preference stops costing you rework every week.
- **Private apps for repeated work.** Construct builds review queues and trackers inside your workspace, and a failed edit leaves the last working build in place.
- **Work that outlives the run.** Reports and files land in the workspace as they are produced, so a run that stops at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29.
- **One team workspace, many channels.** Invite teammates as Owners, Admins, or Members with no member limit, and reach the agent from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord, or its own email inbox.

## What is Grok Bot?

Grok Bot is the agent product from the company behind Grok. You assign work to bots that use a cloud computer, sign into tools, and coordinate across tasks. The [Grok Bot introduction](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) describes parallel bots, shared context, routines learned by demonstration, and access through eligible Grok and Cursor subscriptions. Both products belong in the conversation about an [AI employee for real business work](/blog/ai-employee/).

## Where does Construct give you more control?

Both products give an agent a computer. Construct also lets you decide which tools it reaches, how your team reviews its output, which model it runs on, and what it remembers.

### Connect the tools your business actually uses

Construct finds supported applications through its live integration catalog, including Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable. You can also add custom MCP tools, so the agent reaches a system through an interface your team provides.

An agency that keeps client approvals in a small internal service, for example, can expose the current approval status as a custom tool. Construct then reads it alongside the report files, without waiting for a new catalog entry.

### Give repeated work a small internal app

A report in chat works until several people need to review it. Then you want an owner, a status, a filter, and a place to mark it ready.

Construct builds a private workspace app for that process. The source stays in Files, a successful build opens on the desktop, and a failed edit leaves the last successful build available. Our [internal-tools guide](/blog/build-internal-tools-with-construct/) shows how it works.

A small team gets a focused review queue without starting a separate hosting project. Each workspace app declares its permissions, and a finished app does not inherit every tool the agent that built it can use.

### Bring your own model keys when you need them

Construct Pro supports credentials for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. BYOK can be off, used as an automatic fallback after bundled limits, or set for exclusive use, as the [Construct terms](/terms/) explain. Choosing Construct does not mean giving up xAI: use it through your own key today and switch providers next quarter without moving a single file.

### Correct the context behind the work

An agent that remembers an old preference creates extra work every week. Construct lets you inspect stored memory, correct a fact, or forget it. If a client changes its reporting format, you update the fact once and every later job uses it. The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) explains how, and Activity records each action with a short reason so you can trace what a recent run did.

## A practical example: the weekly client report

Imagine a small agency preparing a Friday account update. This is an illustrative workflow, not a customer case study or a measured comparison.

Save the client list and reporting instructions in the Construct workspace and connect the service that holds the numbers. Ask the agent to read this week's records, compare them with the previous report, and save a draft with links to its sources. Check the first draft by hand.

Once it is right, save the steps as a workflow and put it on the Calendar, where it uses one of Lite's 3 scheduled tasks and runs whether or not anyone is online. Dated reports give next week's job a reference, and a small workspace app can track who has checked each one. The [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) walks through each step.

## Already using Grok Bot? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to cancel Cursor or SuperGrok. Start with the one recurring job your team most needs to see.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test your own model keys and longer runs.
2. **Copy the instructions** you gave your bot into a Construct chat, and move the source files into the workspace.
3. **Connect the apps** the job needs from the integration catalog.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every week with your laptop closed.

There is no import: bots, credentials, and learned memories do not transfer. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read. One thing to set up deliberately: Construct's workflows run in a straight line and do not pause for approval yet, so for anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them.

## When might Grok Bot still fit?

- You already pay for Cursor Pro or SuperGrok and only want to try the included Bot allowance.
- You prefer teaching a routine by demonstrating it rather than describing it.
- You want several named bots coordinating with each other in shared threads.

For recurring team work that needs files, schedules, your own model keys, and memory you can correct, Construct is the better fit.
