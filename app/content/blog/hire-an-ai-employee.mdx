## Why does an AI employee need an onboarding plan?

Because most failures are scoping failures, not model failures. Gartner predicts that "more than 40 percent of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 due to rising costs, unclear business value, or insufficient risk controls," as [The Register reported on June 29, 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/29/ai_agents_fail_a_lot/). Gartner analyst Anushree Verma added that "current models don't have the maturity and agency to autonomously achieve complex business goals or follow nuanced instructions over time."

That is an argument for small, checkable jobs rather than against hiring at all. A new human hire does not get the bank login on day one; they get a task you can check, then a bigger one. The same order works for an agent, and it keeps all three of Gartner's risks in view: you see the cost each week, you see the value job by job, and you add risk only after the earlier stages have held up.

## Week 1: read-only work you can verify

**Set the ground rules first.** Tell the agent how you work and ask it to remember. Memory can be inspected, corrected, or forgotten in the Memories app, so this is safe to adjust later ([AI agent memory](/blog/ai-agent-memory/)).

> Remember these working rules. I run a six-person design studio. Save finished work to Files, not just chat. Cite a source URL for every fact you look up. If you are unsure, ask me instead of guessing. Never send email, post publicly, or change records in any connected app unless I ask for that specific action.

**Give it one research job.** A prospect or vendor list is ideal because every cell can be checked. The [company research guide](/blog/ai-company-research-spreadsheet/) has the full prompt; the short version:

> Research these 10 companies: [list with websites]. For each, record what they sell, pricing model, lowest published monthly price, and whether they offer a free trial, with a source URL for each fact. Write "not found" when you cannot find something. Save it to Files as research/vendors.csv.

**Check everything.** In week 1 you read every row and open every source. You are learning where it is reliable and where your instructions were vague. Correct it in plain language and ask it to remember the correction.

Also look at the agent's email address in the Email app. It is included on every plan, and forwarding a thread from your own account is the easiest way to hand over a job ([AI agent email address](/blog/ai-agent-email-address/)).

## Week 2: one recurring job on a schedule

Pick the job that recurs most predictably, run it on demand once or twice, then schedule it. A weekly report is the classic choice; see the [competitor pricing report guide](/blog/ai-competitor-pricing-report/).

> Every Monday at 8am, run the competitor pricing comparison, save the snapshot and report to Files with the date in the name, and notify me when it is ready.

Scheduled jobs run on Construct's cloud computer with your laptop closed and deliver to the web app with a notification ([running an agent on a schedule](/blog/ai-agent-scheduled-tasks/)). Lite's 3 scheduled tasks are enough for this stage.

This is also the week to connect a channel so you can hand over small jobs from your phone or team chat. Slack, Telegram, and Discord all connect from Settings > Messaging, and each starts locked to "Only people I allow" ([messaging your agent from chat](/blog/ai-agent-telegram-slack-discord/)).

## Week 3: writes to systems you control

Now let it change things, but only in places you own and can undo: notes in your CRM, issues in your tracker, files in a shared folder. Construct finds these apps in its live integration catalog and asks you to connect an account when needed.

> For each client call note I paste this week, add a summary note to the matching HubSpot deal and create a task for each action item I own. Do not change deal stage or amount. Keep a list of every change in Files under changes/ with today's date.

The changes list is your review step: read it after each run and undo anything wrong before the next one. Once a job has run cleanly a few times, save it as a workflow. Current workflows are linear, with no branching, delays, or approval steps ([AI workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/)).

## Week 4: outward-facing work, drafts only

The last stage is work other people see: follow-up emails, client updates, investor updates. Keep every one as a draft you approve.

> Find deals with no activity in 7 days, draft a short follow-up for each as an email draft, and list them with one line on why. Do not send anything until I reply "send" for a specific draft.

## How do you supervise an AI employee?

- **Daily, five questions.** What ran? What did it produce? Did it change anything outside Files? Did it ask me anything? Is anything stuck? Activity answers most of these; it keeps bounded summaries of what the agent did. It is a useful operating record, not a complete immutable audit log ([activity log vs audit log](/blog/ai-agent-activity-log/)).
- **Weekly, read memory.** Open the Memories app and remove anything stale or wrong. A rule saved in week 1 may not fit week 4.
- **Interrupt freely.** You can stop a running job and answer its questions mid-run. Stopping costs nothing; a bad send costs a lot.
- **Expect partial runs.** Finished work lands in Files as it is produced, so a run that dies at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29. Check what was saved before rerunning. Why long runs fail is covered in our [agent task half-life](/blog/agent-task-half-life/) post. {/* TODO: also link /blog/where-ai-agents-fail/ once that research post is live (it needs run data first) */}

## When should you expand on day 30?

**Expand when** a job has run four or more times with only small corrections, you are reviewing rather than redoing, and you can name the next job on your list of ten. That is our rule of thumb, not a measured threshold.

**Change plans when** the limits, not the agent, are what stop you:

- Jobs regularly stop at Lite's 50 steps per task: Starter allows 150.
- You want more than 3 scheduled jobs: Starter has 10, Pro 50.
- You want separate agents for separate areas: Lite has 2, Starter 5, Pro 15.
- You need your own model keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI: that is Pro.

**Rescope a job when** you are still rewriting most of its outputs by week 3: move it back to on demand, tighten the instructions, and save the corrections to memory. Jobs that must react within seconds of an event belong in a trigger-based automation tool; give Construct the recurring work that needs reading, judgment, and a saved result.

**Illustrative example.** A six-person design studio follows this plan. By day 30 the agent runs a Monday vendor report and drafts client follow-ups, which the owner sends after light edits. A third job, reconciling invoices, kept needing corrections, so it went back to on-demand. The studio stays on Lite and revisits Starter when a fourth scheduled job is ready.

## Common mistakes

**Starting with the scariest job.** Customer email in week 1 means every mistake is public. Start read-only.

**Scheduling before it works on demand.** Run a job by hand until the output is right, then schedule it.

**Rules only in chat.** Ask the agent to remember standing rules, then confirm they appear in Memories.

**No owner.** Someone has to read the outputs. An agent nobody reviews is how the Gartner failure modes arrive.
