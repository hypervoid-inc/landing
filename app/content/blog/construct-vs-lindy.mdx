**Lindy is a Slack-first AI teammate priced per user, with credits: Plus is $29.99 a month per user for 3,000 credits. Construct gives each agent its own cloud workspace, with files, a browser, and a terminal, on a flat monthly plan from $9.** Pick Lindy if your team already works in Slack and wants a shared assistant in channels plus a private one for each person's inbox and meetings. Pick Construct if you want a low entry price, work that lives in files you can reopen, and the option to bring your own model keys.

Both are sold as AI employees, but they are built around different places. Lindy lives where your team talks. Construct gives the agent a place to work.

## What is Lindy?

Lindy's documentation opens with a plain definition: "Lindy is an AI employee. It lives in your Slack, connects to your tools, and comes back with the work done." Its [quickstart](https://docs.lindy.ai/start-here/quickstart) describes two modes. In Slack channels, the whole team shares one Lindy you summon with an @mention. In your direct messages, you get a private Lindy that runs your inbox, meetings, and scheduling.

Around that Slack core, Lindy offers [iMessage and SMS](https://docs.lindy.ai/features/imessage-sms) so you can text it from your phone, a Chrome extension, and a web Home page with a chat box. Its [pricing page](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing) lists scheduled Routines, 40+ built-in skills, meeting recording and notes, computer use, "thousands of integrations," model selection across all models, and approvals on every plan.

Lindy has changed shape over the past year. Its [changelog](https://www.lindy.ai/changelog) records Lindy 3.0 in August 2025, with an agent builder and an "Autopilot" cloud computer. The current documentation centers on Lindy Teammate in Slack, which is the product we compare here.

## Methodology

We build Construct. This is our editorial comparison of documented capabilities and published prices, checked on **October 1, 2026**. It is not a hands-on performance benchmark. We read Lindy's pricing page, its documentation, and its changelog. Where Lindy's own pages disagree with each other, we report both. Where a capability is not documented in the pages we reviewed, we say so rather than treating missing documentation as proof it is missing.

## Is Construct cheaper than Lindy?

At the entry level, yes: Construct Lite is $9 a month and Lindy Plus is $29.99 a month per user. That is a comparison of starting prices, not of cost per finished task. The two products meter work differently, so the useful comparison is what each costs for your workload.

These are **USD prices billed monthly**, without annual discounts.

Sources: [Construct pricing](/pricing/), [Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing), and [Lindy plan documentation](https://docs.lindy.ai/pricing). Lindy's docs round its plans to $30, $100, and $200.

Three details in Lindy's billing are worth reading before you buy:

- **Seats.** Lindy's docs say: "Anyone who uses Lindy takes a seat, whether they sign up directly, join your workspace, or @mention Lindy in Slack." New Slack joiners get a 7-day free trial before the seat is billed.
- **Credits.** Lindy's docs put a credit at about one cent. Everyday tasks cost 2 to 250 credits, deep work 250 to 1,000, and major projects 1,000 to 2,500. Credits pool across the workspace and do not roll over.
- **Running out.** When the pool is empty, Lindy pauses until the next cycle. Admins can buy top-ups at $10 per 1,000 credits, and if overages are enabled, the [usage docs](https://docs.lindy.ai/account-billing/usage) say they are charged at twice the regular credit cost.

Lindy's own pages also disagree on two plan limits. The pricing page lists computer use on every plan, while the plan documentation shows it on Pro, Max, and Enterprise only. The plan documentation caps connected inboxes at 2 on Plus, 3 on Pro, and 5 on Max, while the [email drafting guide](https://docs.lindy.ai/features/inbox-management/email-drafting) says "You can connect as many inboxes as you like." Confirm both with Lindy before choosing Plus.

Construct is not unlimited either. Each plan caps agents, steps per task, scheduled tasks, and parallel background jobs, and BYOK adds your own provider charges on Pro. Its plans are priced by agents and usage rather than by the number of people. {/* TODO: verify how many human users can share one Construct plan before stating anything about seats. */}

## Where Construct gives you more control

### The agent has its own place to work

Construct runs each agent in a persistent cloud workspace with files, a live browser, a sandbox terminal, and a native agent email address. A report, a spreadsheet, or a script it writes is saved as a file you can open, edit, and hand back next week. Lindy's work mostly shows up where you already are: a Slack thread, your drafts folder, or an [Artifact](https://docs.lindy.ai/teammate/artifacts) page on a `lindy.link` address.

That matters for recurring work. A weekly brief that starts from last week's file, with last week's corrections, gets better over time. The [product guide](/blog/ai-employee/) shows what a run leaves behind.

### Runs that fail keep their progress

Finished work lands in the Construct workspace as it is produced. A run that dies at step 30 keeps steps 1 to 29, and the next run picks up from what already exists instead of starting over. Workflow run history keeps step results and retry information, and agent steps link back to their chat sessions. Lindy's documentation that we reviewed does not describe what happens to a task that fails partway through.

### Bring your own model keys

Construct Pro accepts your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. Teams with existing provider accounts or credits can use them. Lindy gives every plan access to every model, which is generous, but choosing a model there is a cost decision within Lindy's credits. We did not find a way to supply your own API keys in its docs.

### Correct the context behind the work

Construct lets you inspect stored memory, correct a fact, or forget it, so a changed client preference does not keep resurfacing. The [memory guide](/blog/ai-agent-memory/) covers how that works. Lindy's [memory](https://docs.lindy.ai/teammate/memory) is also open to inspection, as files with version history, so this is shared ground rather than a Construct advantage. The difference is in scope: Construct's memory sits alongside the workspace files and workflows the agent uses.

### Reach it from more places than Slack

Construct accepts work from the web desktop, Slack, Telegram, Discord slash commands, and its own email inbox. If requests arrive by email from clients or in a Telegram group, the agent can take them there.

## Can Construct work in Slack like Lindy?

Partly. You can message Construct directly from Slack, and a workflow can post results to a Slack channel through a connected-app action, as our [workflow guide](/blog/ai-workflow-automation/) shows with a weekly competitor summary. {/* TODO: verify whether Construct responds to @mentions in shared Slack channels before claiming channel parity. */}

Lindy goes further inside Slack. It is designed as a shared teammate in channels and a private assistant in each person's DMs, with approval buttons posted in threads. If Slack is the whole interface for your team, Lindy fits that more closely.

## Where Lindy is the better choice

- **Your team runs on Slack.** Lindy's shared channel teammate and per-person DM assistant are the core of the product, not an add-on channel.
- **You want approval before every write.** Lindy's integrations can be set to require approval in Slack before writing, per action. Construct workflows are linear today and have no approval gates, so a scheduled job that emails customers runs without a stop for review.
- **Inbox and meeting help out of the box.** Lindy ships email labeling, drafting into your Gmail or Outlook drafts folder, follow-up bumps, meeting recording, meeting prep, and a daily brief as ready-made features. On Construct you describe that work yourself.
- **Texting from your phone.** Lindy supports iMessage and SMS. Construct reaches you through Slack, Telegram, Discord, email, and the web, not text messages.
- **Compliance.** Lindy lists SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA, and PIPEDA, and Enterprise adds a signed BAA, SSO and SCIM, and audit logs. Construct's Activity summaries are useful operating records, not a complete immutable audit log.
- **Memory history.** Every save to Lindy's memory is a version you can restore, including deleted files.
- **Trying it for free.** Lindy's homepage offers $50 of free credits with no card required.

## Moving from Lindy to Construct

There is no one-click import, so move one job at a time.

**What moves over:** your instructions and Routine prompts (copy them as plain text), the contents of memory files you copy from Lindy's Memory page, reports and documents you keep, and your integrations, which you reconnect to Construct's catalog or add as custom MCP tools. A Lindy Routine's trigger, prompt, and destination map onto a Construct workflow and a Calendar schedule, as long as the trigger is time-based.

**What does not move over:** Lindy's credits, its built-in inbox features such as labeling and follow-up bumps, meeting recordings, iMessage threads, Artifact pages on `lindy.link`, and approval rules. Event triggers such as "an email received" need to be rebuilt, and Construct workflows are linear, so a Routine that relied on approvals should become an on-demand workflow a person starts after reviewing.

Start with the job that costs you the most credits or the most checking. Run it manually on Construct a few times, compare the saved result with what Lindy produced, and only then put it on a schedule.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Lindy** if your team already lives in Slack, several people want their own inbox and meeting assistant, and you value built-in approval before writes. Budget per person, and model a busy month against the credit pool.

**Choose Construct** if you are a solo founder or small team that wants the lowest entry price, work saved as files in a workspace the agent returns to, runs that keep progress when they fail, requests from email or Telegram as well as Slack, and your own model keys on Pro.

**For a solo founder,** the deciding question is where your work arrives. If it is mostly your own inbox and calendar and you want those handled with minimal setup, Lindy's Plus seat does that directly. If it is research, reports, and recurring jobs that cross several apps, Construct Lite at $9 is the cheaper place to start, and Starter adds the scheduling headroom.

Pick the report or inbox chore that keeps landing in your evening. Give Construct the inputs, describe what a finished result looks like, and check the saved file.
