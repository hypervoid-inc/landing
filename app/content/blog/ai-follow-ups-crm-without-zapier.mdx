Construct handles customer follow-ups from one prompt for $9 a month: it finds the deals that have gone quiet, reads each one's last email thread, drafts a follow-up that answers what the customer actually said, and logs a note in your CRM, with every email kept as a draft until you approve it. Zapier's paid plans start at $29.99 a month billed monthly, and a Zap runs from a trigger with the filters, Paths, and templates or AI steps you build and configure ([Zapier pricing](https://zapier.com/pricing)).

For founders and small teams, Construct is the better tool for follow-ups, because the hard part is judgment, not field mapping: which deals actually need a nudge, what the customer last said, and what a sensible next message is. Construct reads and decides, costs less than a third of Zapier Professional to start, and keeps a dated record of every draft and CRM change in your workspace.

## Why do this in Construct?

- **It decides which deals need a nudge.** The agent reads each deal and thread instead of following rules you have to build, and it skips the customer who wrote "back after the 20th."
- **Drafts written from the real thread.** Each follow-up refers to the last thing the customer said, in your voice, under the length you set.
- **Call notes become CRM updates.** Paste notes or forward them to the agent's email address, and it adds the summary, creates your tasks, and flags anything that conflicts with HubSpot.
- **A record of every change.** Each run leaves a dated follow-up table and a changes file in Files, alongside Activity.
- **Rules you can read and fix.** "Never change stage or amount" and "never send without my send" live in the Memories app, where you can correct or remove them.
- **Every weekday at 8am, without you.** The Calendar runs the draft job on Construct's cloud computer with your laptop closed. Lite includes 3 scheduled tasks.

## What do you need?

- **A Construct workspace.** Lite at $9 a month works for a handful of deals; Starter at $59, with 150 steps per task, suits a weekly pass over a larger pipeline. Or try Pro free for 7 days. See [pricing](/pricing/).
- **Your CRM and email connected** from Construct's live integration catalog. HubSpot, Airtable, and Notion are in the catalog, as is Gmail. The agent asks you to connect an account when it first needs one.
- **A definition of "needs a follow-up."** For example: open deals with no logged activity in 7 days.

## Before you start

Set the job up this way and nothing reaches a customer by surprise:

- **Keep customer email as drafts.** Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so the standing rule in step 2 and a draft-only schedule are your gate. You send, or you reply "send" for a specific draft.
- **Run it on a rhythm, not on every event.** Construct runs on demand or from the Calendar, as often as every 15 minutes, rather than the instant a deal changes. A weekday morning pass is the natural cadence for follow-ups.
- **Read the changes file.** A cancelled run stops later steps, but a note already written stays written. The dated changes file shows exactly what to undo.
- **Send operational email, not campaigns.** The agent's native inbox has a daily sending limit enforced on the server. For customer follow-ups, drafts in your own Gmail are the better route anyway.
- **Check your CRM's catalog actions first.** Step 1 lists what the agent can do in HubSpot and Gmail. If your CRM is not in the integration catalog, the agent may use a browser instead, which is slower than a structured action.


## How do you set it up?

### 1. Connect the apps and check what the agent can do

> Connect HubSpot and Gmail. Then list the actions you can use in each one that matter for follow-ups: reading deals and contacts, adding notes, setting tasks or next-step dates, reading email threads, and creating drafts. Do not change anything yet.

This tells you, in your own account, what is possible before you rely on it. {/* TODO: confirm which HubSpot actions (read deals, add note, create task, update property) and Gmail actions (read thread, create draft, send) are available in the catalog today */}

### 2. Give the agent standing rules

Put the guardrails in memory once, rather than in every prompt. You can inspect, correct, or forget them later in the Memories app ([AI agent memory](/blog/ai-agent-memory/)).

> Remember these rules for customer follow-ups. Never send an email to a customer unless I reply "send" for that specific draft. Never change a deal stage or amount. You may add notes and set next-step dates in HubSpot. Write follow-ups in plain language, under 120 words, referring to the last thing the customer said. If a thread shows the customer said no or asked us to stop, do not draft anything; flag it instead.

### 3. Build the follow-up list, read-only

> In HubSpot, find open deals with no logged activity in the last 7 days. For each, find the most recent email thread with the main contact in Gmail. Save a table to Files as followups/ with today's date as the file name, with columns: deal, contact, last activity date, what the customer last said in one sentence, suggested next step, and a link to the deal. Do not draft or change anything yet.

Read the table. This is where you catch a deal that is stalled for a good reason, such as a customer on holiday, and remove it.

### 4. Draft the follow-ups

> For each deal still in followups/ with today's date, draft a follow-up email as a Gmail draft from my account, following my follow-up rules. Then list the drafts here with one line on why you chose that angle.

Open Gmail, read the drafts, edit them in your voice, and send the ones you like yourself. If you prefer the agent to send, reply "send" with the deal names; the rule in step 2 makes that explicit. For messages where it is fine for the customer to know an agent wrote them, the agent can use its own native address instead ([giving an agent its own email](/blog/ai-agent-email-address/)).

### 5. Update the CRM after you have sent

> For each follow-up I sent today, add a note to the HubSpot deal: "Follow-up sent [date]: [one-line summary]". Set the next-step date to 5 business days from today. Do not change stage or amount. Save a list of what you changed to followups/ with today's date and "-changes" in the name.

The changes file is your record of every CRM write the agent made, alongside Activity.

### 6. Turn call notes into CRM updates

This is the CRM chore most people skip. Paste notes into chat, or forward them to the agent's email address from your own account.

> Here are my notes from today's call with Acme: [paste notes]. Update the Acme deal in HubSpot: add the notes as a summary, create a task for each action item I own with a due date, and list anything in the notes that conflicts with what is already in HubSpot. Do not change stage or amount.

### 7. Put the list-building on a schedule

Once steps 3 and 4 have produced good drafts for a few weeks, save them as a workflow and schedule it. Current workflows are linear, with no branching, delays, or approval steps, so keep the schedule draft-only.

> Save steps 3 and 4 as a workflow called "Weekday follow-up drafts" and schedule it every weekday at 8am. End with an in-app notification listing the drafts. Never send.

You then review and send each morning. Lite includes 3 scheduled tasks, Starter 10, and Pro 50.

## What result should you get?

Each run leaves a dated follow-up table in Files, Gmail drafts waiting for you, and, after step 5, a dated list of CRM changes. Nothing reaches a customer until you send it.

**Illustrative example.** A two-person consultancy has 14 open deals. On Monday the agent flags 5 with no activity in 7 days, and one of them is a client who wrote "back after the 20th", so the owner deletes that row. The agent drafts 4 emails that each refer to the last thing the client said. The owner edits two, sends all four, and asks the agent to log notes and set next-step dates. The owner reviews drafts instead of reading every thread from scratch.

## How do you supervise it?

- **Drafts first, always, for customer email.** Your standing rules and your "send" are the gate, so keep the scheduled job draft-only.
- **Read the changes file.** Every CRM write is listed there. Spot-check a few deals in HubSpot after the first runs.
- **Keep stage and amount yours.** Deal stage and value drive forecasts; letting the agent add notes and dates is lower risk.
- **Use Activity when something looks wrong.** It keeps bounded summaries of what the agent did. It is a useful operating record, not a complete immutable audit log.

## Where does Zapier fit?

Zapier is built for fixed, event-triggered jobs: "when a form is submitted, create a contact in HubSpot and send the welcome template" runs from the trigger the same way every time. It connects to over 10,000 apps ([Zapier apps](https://zapier.com/apps)), offers Paths and filters on paid plans, and has a Human in the Loop step that pauses a Zap for review on Professional, Team, and Enterprise ([Zapier help: Human in the Loop](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/38733184458765-Use-Human-in-the-Loop-to-pause-Zaps-pending-human-review)). If you already run Zaps like that, keep them, and give Construct the follow-ups that need reading. ## Already using Zapier? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to turn off a single Zap. Keep the ones that create records on events, and hand Construct the follow-ups.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** if your pipeline is large.
2. **Connect HubSpot and Gmail** from the integration catalog and run the step 1 prompt.
3. **Paste the step 2 rules,** adding any field your Zaps own so the agent leaves it alone.
4. **Run steps 3 and 4 once** and check the table and drafts.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** for weekday mornings, draft-only.

There is no import: Zaps do not convert into Construct workflows. Describe the job in plain language instead, using the prompts above.

## Troubleshooting

**The agent drafted a follow-up to someone who said no.** Check the rule in step 2 is saved in Memories, and add the specific phrase the customer used.

**Drafts do not appear in Gmail.** Confirm the Gmail connection and that the draft action is available, or ask for drafts in Files instead.

**HubSpot notes landed on the wrong deal.** Ask the agent to match deals by ID, not by company name, and include the deal link in every table.

**A Zap and the agent both updated the same field.** Give each one its own fields: the Zap owns record creation on events, the agent owns notes and next steps.
