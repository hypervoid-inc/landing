**You can get an always-on agent without ChatGPT Pro for $0 to $50 a month.** If you already pay $20 for Claude, Google AI Pro, or Cursor, try Claude Cowork, Gemini Spark, or Grok Bot first; to start free, look at Meta Muse or Manus; for a small team's recurring work, Construct starts at $9, Lindy at $29.99 per user, and Viktor at $50 per workspace; and OpenClaw is free if you host it yourself.

ChatGPT Dots, launched on September 29, 2026, are OpenAI's always-on agents. Each one has its own cloud computer and keeps working when you close the laptop, but your first dot only comes with ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium. BGR's launch report puts that at $200 a month for one person, though some guides say $100. This guide compares nine alternatives on price, on **which subscription each one needs**, and on what keeps running after you close the laptop. We checked them all on October 1, 2026.

## What are ChatGPT Dots, and what do they cost?

A dot is an agent inside ChatGPT that you give a goal. It works toward that goal on its own cloud computer and browser, uses the apps you connect, and comes back when it needs a decision. [Decrypt's DevDay roundup](https://decrypt.co/379584/openai-ai-agents-computers-devday-2026-everything-announced) quotes OpenAI calling them "remarkably capable, always-on agents."

What launch coverage agrees on:

- **Its own computer.** Each dot runs on GPT-6 Astra with a dedicated cloud computer and browser that you can open to watch what it is doing ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/), [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2272332/openai-devday-2026-announcements/)).
- **A large app catalog.** Dots connect to more than 4,000 apps through OpenAI's plugins ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/)).
- **Several ways to reach it.** You can message or call a dot in ChatGPT on desktop, web, and mobile, or message it in Slack and Microsoft Teams. Texting is listed as coming soon ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday)).
- **Approvals by default.** Dots start with built-in rules on when to act alone and when to ask. You can add Custom Rules that allow, block, or require approval for specific actions ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday)). When you are away, a dot can do "proactive research," but only with read-only app access.
- **Scheduled work.** You can schedule runs, such as a morning review of your calendar, and manage active, paused, and completed runs ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/openai-dots)).

### Which ChatGPT plan do you need for Dots?

Your first dot is included in ChatGPT Pro and Business Premium, and Free and Plus users do not get one ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379584/openai-ai-agents-computers-devday-2026-everything-announced)). Reports disagree on which Pro tier counts. OpenAI now sells Pro at $100, $200, and a new $500 tier. [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2272332/openai-devday-2026-announcements/) says dots "are only available on ChatGPT's Pro 200 and Business Premium plans" and are "not currently available on the cheapest of the Pro tier, which costs $100 a month." [Fello AI's DevDay guide](https://felloai.com/openai-dots/) lists Pro 100 as including a dot. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/openai-takes-on-metas-muse-with-new-dot-agents-and-unveils-a-potential-google-workspace-competitor/) says Pro and Business Premium users get one free dot but discusses only the $200 and $500 plans.

OpenAI's own pages blocked our fetch, so we could not settle it at the source. Budget $200 a month for one person until OpenAI's pricing page says otherwise.

Three other terms matter for a buyer. Dots usage does not count against plan allowances for the first month, and OpenAI has not published what happens after that. Extra dots and more capacity are planned "at prices OpenAI has not disclosed" ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/)). And Pro subscribers in the European Economic Area, Switzerland, and the UK are excluded for now, while Business Premium users get dots in every supported ChatGPT region.

## Why do people look for a ChatGPT Dots alternative?

The launch drew a lot of attention: the Hacker News thread for [OpenAI's Dots announcement](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49896604) collected 731 points and 619 comments within a day. Three objections came up again and again.

**The subscription.** "The average consumer doesn't have a $100+ ChatGPT Pro subscription though," [one commenter wrote](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49897885). For a small team, a $200 plan for each person who wants a dot adds up quickly, and there is no Plus or Free route.

**Where you live.** If you pay for Pro in the UK, Switzerland, or the EEA, you cannot get a dot on that plan today, whatever you are willing to spend.

**How cautious it is.** Approval by default is a real safety feature, and many teams will want it. It also changes how the product feels. One early user [graded Dots a B-](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49904198) after two hours, writing that "it won't accept rules in it's permissions UI that give it broad authorization to do things for you" and that "if you're looking for an openclaw like agent, this isn't it at all." OpenAI itself says "Dots can still make mistakes, so always review consequential work" ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday)).

None of these make Dots a bad product. They make it a specific one: an OpenAI agent for people already on the top ChatGPT plans, outside Europe, who want safety rules decided for them by default.

## If you already pay for an AI subscription

These three are the cheapest route for many people because the agent comes with a plan they already have. Check the fine print on where each one runs.

### Claude Cowork

**Best for:** teams already paying for Claude who want scheduled work that runs without their computer.

Cowork is Anthropic's mode for handing Claude "complex, multi-step tasks" to carry out on your behalf ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13345190-get-started-with-claude-cowork)). Tasks run in the cloud, in beta, with sessions saved to your account. Claude can open sites, click, type, and fill forms in a browser, and you choose which connectors it may use. Scheduled tasks run hourly, daily, weekly, or on weekdays, and they "run on their cadence even when your computer is asleep or the Claude Desktop app is closed" ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13854387-schedule-recurring-tasks-in-claude-cowork)). You pick an approval mode: Manual, Auto with safety checks, or Skip.

**Price:** Cowork and scheduled tasks are on every paid Claude plan. Pro is $20 billed monthly, Max starts at $100, and Team standard seats are $25 per seat billed monthly ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)).

**Limits:** when a task needs a local file or your own browser, Claude reaches it through the Claude Desktop app, so that part still depends on your machine. Anthropic says that from October 6, 2026, new Cowork tasks run in the cloud and the "Only on your computer" setting goes away. We found no published cap on scheduled tasks in the help article we read.

### Gemini Spark

**Best for:** people who live in Gmail, Calendar, and Drive and are outside Europe.

Google calls Spark "your 24/7 personal AI agent" and says it "works in the background 24/7, even if your phone and laptop are turned off" ([Gemini Spark](https://gemini.google/overview/agent/spark/)). It connects to Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, YouTube, and Maps, with those connections off by default. Schedules can use time-based or conditional triggers, and Google says it is "designed to check with you before taking major actions."

**Price:** Spark is included with Google AI Pro and Ultra, not with Google AI Plus ([Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/)). [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/googles-agentic-ai-gemini-spark-is-now-available-on-the-20-pro-tier-in-the-us/) reported on July 27, 2026 that it had reached the "$20-a-month Google AI Pro tier in the US," having launched on Ultra.

**Limits:** you must be 18 or older, in a supported country. Chrome Unboxed reported that the rollout "excludes the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and Nigeria" and that work and school accounts were still out. Google's own page now mentions "select business users," so check your account type before you plan around it. Spark works with Google's apps; for a team whose tools are mostly elsewhere, it is a narrower fit.

### Grok Bot

**Best for:** anyone already paying for Cursor or SuperGrok.

Grok Bot gives you named Bots that share "a persistent cloud computer with a browser, filesystem, and terminal," and work continues "while your laptop is closed" ([Grok Bot docs](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview)). Bots can learn a path, save it as a skill, and rerun it on a schedule. They come back when something needs your approval and can leave drafts for you to approve before they are sent.

**Price:** there is no standalone plan. Access is included with every paid individual Cursor plan and with Cursor Teams, or through a linked SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, SuperGrok Heavy, or X Premium+ subscription ([Cursor help](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/plans)). Cursor's individual plan is $20 a month and lists "Grok Bot access" ([Cursor pricing](https://cursor.com/pricing)). Usage resets weekly, and extra usage can continue as on-demand usage if you turn that on.

## If you want to start free

### Meta Muse and Muse for Small Business

**Best for:** small businesses in the US or Canada whose customers and ads live on Instagram and Facebook.

Meta launched Muse on September 8, 2026. It "runs on its own dedicated computer in the cloud," can "open a browser, fill out forms," and "keeps working after people close the app" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)). On September 29, Meta added [Muse for Small Business](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-small-business/), with connectors for Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom, plus Instagram analytics, Facebook Pages, and Meta ad accounts. Meta says "nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval."

**Price:** Meta says Muse is "free for most of what people need, with subscription plans for people who want to do more." Neither announcement lists the paid plan prices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/meta-is-expanding-its-ai-agent-muse-to-small-businesses/) also describes "a subscription plan" without a figure).

**Limits:** the personal Muse app is rolling out in the US; Muse for Small Business is available in the US and Canada. It is strongest when the business runs on Meta's platforms. If most of your work is in a CRM, a code repository, or internal files, check the connector list against your stack first.

### Manus

**Best for:** research, analysis, and build tasks you hand over one at a time, with an optional always-on machine.

Manus runs each task in its own sandbox. Manus 2.0, announced September 28, 2026, turned scheduled tasks into Automations that can also start "when something happens in a connected service" ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0)). The optional Cloud Computer is a persistent virtual machine for work that needs to keep running.

**Price:** the free plan has 300 daily refresh credits, 1 concurrent task, and 2 scheduled tasks. Pro starts at $20 a month for 4,000 credits with 20 scheduled tasks, and Team starts at $20 per seat ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/11711111-what-is-the-current-membership-pricing-for-manus)). The Cloud Computer is billed separately: Standard is $30 a month and Advanced is $50 ([Manus Help Center](https://help.manus.im/en/articles/15392078-understanding-cloud-computer-plans-and-billing)).

**Limits:** credit use varies by task. If you stop paying for the Cloud Computer, "the persistent virtual machine will be shut down" and its files, tools, and databases are deleted, though outputs delivered in chat remain. Back up before you cancel.

## If you want an AI employee for a small team

These sell their own plan rather than bundling the agent into a chat subscription. That makes them easier to budget for a team, because you are not buying a premium chatbot seat for each person who wants an agent.

### Construct

**Best for:** small teams whose recurring work crosses several apps and should leave files behind.

Construct gives each agent a persistent cloud workspace with files, live browser runs, a sandbox terminal, a native agent email address, and a Calendar of scheduled jobs. It reaches Gmail, Notion, Linear, GitHub, HubSpot, Airtable, and more through a live integration catalog, and you can add custom MCP tools when a connection does not exist. You can assign work from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord slash commands, or the agent's own inbox. Memory can be inspected, corrected, or forgotten.

Finished work lands in the workspace as it is produced. If a run dies at step 30, steps 1 to 29 are still there, and the next run starts from what exists. A job that works can be saved as a workflow and scheduled on the Calendar, and it runs from the cloud workspace rather than from your laptop.

**Price:** Lite is $9 a month for up to 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and agent email. Starter is $59 with 6x Lite usage, up to 5 agents, 150 steps per task, and 10 scheduled tasks. Pro is $299 with 32x Lite usage, up to 15 agents, up to 1,000 steps per task, 50 scheduled tasks, and bring-your-own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, and xAI. Pro has a 7-day trial ([Construct pricing](/pricing/)).

**Limits:** Dots is ahead on guardrails. Construct has no equivalent of Custom Rules and no mandatory approval gate before external actions, and workflows are linear, with no branching, delays, or approval steps. Supervision comes from asking for drafts and reviewing early runs. Activity keeps useful summaries and bounded tool records, not a complete audit log. Microsoft Teams is not one of its channels, and Dots' 4,000-app plugin catalog is larger than Construct's integration list. Our [Construct vs ChatGPT Dots comparison](/blog/construct-vs-openai-dots/) covers the head-to-head.

### Lindy

**Best for:** teams whose work already happens in Slack.

Lindy is an AI teammate that answers in Slack threads, over iMessage, and in your inbox. It runs scheduled routines, can "drive the browser like a person would" when a tool has no integration, and says "nothing irreversible happens without your approval" ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/)).

**Price:** Plus is $29.99 per user per month with 3,000 credits, Pro is $99.99 with 15,000, and Max is $199.99 with 35,000. Each seat's credits go into one shared pool ([Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing)).

**Limits:** billing is per user, so a five-person team on Plus pays about $150 a month. Lindy's pricing page lists computer use on every plan, while its homepage says browser driving is "Pro and up." Confirm with Lindy before you choose Plus for browser work.

### Viktor

**Best for:** a team that wants one shared coworker in Slack or Microsoft Teams without per-seat billing.

Viktor describes itself as "an AI employee that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams" and connects to more than 3,200 tools. It has "his own computer in the cloud where he writes and runs code to complete tasks," can schedule recurring tasks, and asks for confirmation "before high-stakes actions like sending emails or deploying to production" ([Viktor](https://viktor.com/)).

**Price:** team plans start at $50 a month for 20,000 credits. Pricing is per workspace, not per seat, so the team shares one credit pool, and unused monthly credits roll over for one month. New workspaces get $100 in credits without a card ([Viktor pricing](https://viktor.com/pricing)).

**Limits:** it lives in Slack or Teams, so it suits teams that already work there. Every task draws on the shared credit pool, so price a busy month before you commit.

## If you want to run it yourself

### OpenClaw, self-hosted or on MyClaw

**Best for:** technical owners who want full control of the agent, its models, and its data.

OpenClaw is an MIT-licensed personal agent stewarded by a non-profit foundation. It has "no subscription. No hosted tier," runs on Mac, Windows, or Linux, and talks to you on WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Slack, Signal, iMessage, and more. You bring the model access you want ([OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/)).

If you want it always on without running a server, a third-party host such as MyClaw offers "fully managed cloud hosting for OpenClaw" with "always-on uptime, automatic updates, daily backups, and an isolated private instance." MyClaw's Lite plan is $199 a year, about $16.60 a month, and "AI token usage isn't included in the subscription" ([MyClaw pricing](https://myclaw.ai/pricing)).

**Limits:** self-hosting is free software but not free time: installing, updating, securing, and keeping it online is your job. On a host, you pay for hosting and for model tokens separately. MyClaw is an independent service, not part of the OpenClaw project. Our comparison of [building your own AI agent](/blog/construct-vs-diy/) covers that tradeoff.

## What does an always-on agent cost for a three-person team?

This is an illustrative calculation using list prices at monthly billing, not a measured test. Usage allowances differ widely, so a lower total does not mean equal work or a lower cost per finished task.

Imagine a three-person agency. It wants one agent that checks three competitor pricing pages every Monday, drafts a client status update from the shared inbox, and saves both to a folder the whole team can open.

- **ChatGPT Dots:** one person on ChatGPT Pro 200 is $200 a month, and that dot works on that person's behalf. Giving each person a dot on Pro is $600. Business Premium seat pricing was not confirmed at the source.
- **Construct Lite:** $9 a month covers up to 2 agents and 3 scheduled tasks, enough for two weekly jobs. The files land in one shared workspace.
- **Viktor:** $50 a month for the workspace, shared by all three people.
- **Lindy Plus:** $29.99 per user, so $89.97 for three people.
- **Claude, Google, or Cursor at $20:** $20 per person who needs the agent, if the job fits that product's apps and region.

The cheapest option on paper is the one you already pay for. After that, the questions that decide the bill are whether the whole team can share one agent, and what happens at the limit in a busy month.

## Which ChatGPT Dots alternative should you choose?

- **If you already pay $20 for Claude, Google AI Pro, or Cursor,** try Claude Cowork, Gemini Spark, or Grok Bot first. It costs nothing extra to find out whether the job fits.
- **If you are in the UK, Switzerland, or the EEA,** Dots on Pro and Gemini Spark are both out for now. Claude Cowork, Grok Bot, Manus, Construct, Lindy, Viktor, and OpenClaw do not list the same exclusions on the pages we read; check your country at sign-up.
- **If you want to spend nothing to start,** try Manus's free plan for one-off tasks, or Meta Muse if you are in the US or Canada and your business runs on Instagram and Facebook.
- **If a small team needs one agent with shared files and a schedule,** look at Construct from $9 or Viktor from $50 per workspace.
- **If your team lives in Slack,** Lindy and Viktor are built around it. Construct also accepts Slack messages.
- **If you need Microsoft Teams,** Viktor and Dots list it as a channel. Construct does not, so check each vendor's channel list before you choose.
- **If you want approvals and safety rules decided for you,** Dots is the most cautious by design, and Muse, Lindy, and Viktor also ask before sending or spending. Construct relies on you asking for drafts and reviewing runs.
- **If you want to own the whole stack,** self-host OpenClaw, or pay a host such as MyClaw to keep it running.
- **If you already have ChatGPT Pro 200,** your first dot is included. Try it before you add anything.

## Give Construct one recurring job

Pick one job that repeats every week and crosses two or three tools, such as the Monday competitor check or a client status draft. Give Construct the inputs, ask it to save the result as a file, and check the first run by hand. Once it is right, put it on the Calendar. Lite at $9 includes 3 scheduled tasks, and the seven-day Pro trial lets you test deeper runs. See the [current plans](/pricing/) for limits.
