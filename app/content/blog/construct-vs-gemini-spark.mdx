Construct gives your team an AI employee with its own workspace, files, schedules, and email address from $9 a month, and it connects to Gmail and Google Calendar alongside HubSpot, Notion, and the rest of your stack. Gemini Spark comes with Google AI Pro ($19.99 a month in the US) or Ultra (from $99.99), works only on a personal Google account rather than a Workspace work or school account, and Google lists it as unavailable in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and Nigeria.

For most founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. It costs less to start, does not ask you to move the business onto a personal Google account, keeps finished work in a workspace the whole team can open, and reaches the tools outside Google that your recurring jobs touch. Already paying for Google AI Pro? Keep Spark for personal errands and give Construct the recurring team work. The two run side by side.

## Why choose Construct over Gemini Spark?

- **$9 a month, with no Google subscription first.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents, 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address.
- **Built for a business account, not a personal one.** Construct connects Gmail and Google Calendar through its live integration catalog with the account you authorize. Spark needs a personal Google Account today.
- **One workspace for the whole team, no seats.** Invite teammates with Owner, Admin, or Member roles. A team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan.
- **Google plus the rest of your stack.** The same catalog covers Notion, Linear, Jira, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable, and you can add your own MCP tools.
- **Work that outlives the chat.** Reports and digests land in workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 still leaves steps 1 to 29 behind.
- **Your model, and more ways to reach it.** Pro runs on your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI. Message the agent on the web, in Slack, Telegram, or Discord, or by email.

## What is Gemini Spark?

Gemini Spark is Google's "24/7 personal AI agent" inside the Gemini app. Google announced it on May 19, 2026 as a cloud-based agent on Gemini 3.5, starting with a beta for US Google AI Ultra subscribers ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/next-evolution-gemini-app/)).

You give it tasks, teach it skills, and set schedules with "time-based or conditional triggers" ([Gemini Spark](https://gemini.google/overview/agent/spark/)). It works natively with Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, YouTube, and Google Maps. A June update added a beta macOS app, connected apps including Canva, Dropbox, Instacart, OpenTable, and Zillow Rentals, and custom MCP connections ([Google, June 30, 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-spark-updates-june-2026/)).

Google's help page sets the working limits ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215?hl=en)):

- **Account:** you must be 18 or over, have Keep Activity on, and "sign in to the Gemini app with a personal Google Account." Work and school accounts are not supported.
- **Concurrency:** up to 15 tasks running at a time.
- **Approvals:** Spark asks before sending communications, modifying data, making purchases, submitting forms, or signing into websites.
- **Browser:** your own Chrome, which needs your device on, or a remote browser that pauses at sign-in steps until you step in.

For a wider field, our guide to [Gemini Spark alternatives](/blog/gemini-spark-alternatives/) compares seven options. This page is the head-to-head with Construct.

## Where does Construct give you more control?

Spark is deep inside Google. Construct is built for a business that runs on Google and several other tools, with work a team shares.

### It works when your business runs on a Workspace account

Most small companies on Google keep email, calendar, and files under Workspace work accounts, which Spark does not support today. Google said on May 19 that "Gemini Spark in Google Workspace will be available soon in preview for business customers" ([Google Workspace blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/workspace/workspace-updates/)); we found no general availability date. One Hacker News commenter put it this way in July: "many new Gemini functionalities are only accessible using a consumer account instead of a Workspace account" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48994600)).

Construct connects to Gmail and Google Calendar through its live integration catalog, with whichever account you authorize. {/* TODO: verify whether a Google Workspace admin must approve Construct's Gmail and Google Calendar connection, and say so here. */} Our guide to [letting an AI agent use your Gmail safely](/blog/ai-agent-gmail-access-safely/) covers the scopes to grant and what to keep as drafts.

### One workspace for the team, not one agent per person

Construct's [team workspaces](/use-cases/team-workspaces/) hold the agent, files, apps, and conversations together, and teammates join with roles instead of borrowing a login. Every run saves its output to workspace files as it goes, so the digest your colleague asked for on Tuesday is still there on Friday.

### Google plus the rest of the stack

Spark's connected apps lean toward personal life: Instacart, OpenTable, Zillow Rentals. Construct's catalog includes business tools such as Notion, Linear, Jira, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable, and you can add custom MCP tools. If the job is "read Gmail, update HubSpot, save a weekly report," Construct is built for it.

### More channels, and its own email address

You reach Spark in the Gemini app on mobile, Mac, and the web. Construct takes work from the web, Slack, Telegram, Discord, and its own email address. Forward a supplier thread from your own address and the agent starts work on it, without the job running through a personal inbox.

### A choice of model

Spark runs on Gemini. Construct Pro can run on your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI, either as a fallback after bundled limits or for every run, as the [Construct terms](/terms/) explain. Your procedures and files stay put if you change providers.

## Already using Spark? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to give up Spark. Start with the job that touches a work account, a teammate, or a tool outside Google.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test your own model keys and longer runs.
2. **Copy the instructions and skill** you set up in Spark into a Construct chat, and save the Docs or Sheets it uses into the workspace.
3. **Connect Gmail, Google Calendar, and the other apps** the job needs from the integration catalog.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every weekday whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Spark's skills, schedules, and remote browser data do not transfer, and Google's help page says turning Spark off deletes its remote browser and code execution data, so save anything you need first. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read. One thing to set up deliberately: Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so for replies and anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them.

## When might Spark still fit?

- You run a one-person business from a personal Gmail account and already pay for Google AI Pro.
- The work lives almost entirely in Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Maps, where Spark's native access goes deepest.
- You want confirmations before every send and purchase built in, or need the agent to use your local Chrome or Mac.

For recurring work on a business account, shared with a team, and crossing Google and other tools, Construct is the better fit.

Choose Construct. It starts at $9 instead of $19.99 to $99.99, connects to Gmail and Google Calendar on the account you authorize, keeps every finished file in a workspace your team shares, and reaches the business tools outside Google.
