Construct vs Gemini Spark comes down to price and accounts: Construct gives your team an AI employee with its own workspace, files, schedules, and email address from $9 a month, while Spark needs Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month (US only for Pro) or Ultra from $99.99, and works only on a personal Google account. Google also lists Spark as unavailable in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and Nigeria.

For most founders and small teams, Construct is the better choice. It costs less to start, does not ask you to move the business onto a personal Google account, keeps finished work in a workspace the whole team can open, and reaches the tools outside Google that your recurring jobs touch. Already paying for Google AI Pro? Keep Spark for personal errands and give Construct the recurring team work. The two run side by side.

## Why choose Construct over Gemini Spark?

Construct is cheaper to start, works with the Google account your business actually uses, and is built for a team rather than one person.

- **$9 a month, with no Google subscription first.** Construct Lite includes 2 agents (Construct and one more), 50 steps per task, 3 scheduled tasks, and an agent email address.
- **Built for a business account, not a personal one.** Construct connects Gmail and Google Calendar through its live integration catalog with the account you authorize. Spark needs a personal Google Account today.
- **One workspace for the whole team, no seats.** Invite teammates with Owner, Admin, or Member roles. A team workspace has no member limit, and everyone's work draws on the owner's plan.
- **Google plus the rest of your stack.** The same catalog covers Notion, Linear, Jira, GitHub, HubSpot, and Airtable, and you can add your own MCP tools.
- **Work that outlives the chat.** Reports and digests land in workspace files as the agent produces them, so a run that stops at step 30 still leaves steps 1 to 29 behind.
- **Your model, and more ways to reach it.** Pro runs on your own keys for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, or xAI. Message the agent on the web, in Slack or Telegram, with Discord slash commands, or by email.

## What is Gemini Spark?

Gemini Spark is Google's "24/7 personal AI agent" inside the Gemini app: you give it tasks, teach it skills, and set schedules, and it keeps working in the cloud while your devices are off ([Gemini Spark](https://gemini.google/overview/agent/spark/)). Google announced it on May 19, 2026 as a cloud-based agent on Gemini 3.5, starting with a beta for US Google AI Ultra subscribers ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/next-evolution-gemini-app/)).

Schedules use "time-based or conditional triggers," and Spark works natively with Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, YouTube, and Google Maps ([Gemini Spark](https://gemini.google/overview/agent/spark/)). Google's changelog adds Google Tasks and Keep, custom connected apps through MCP server URLs, a Mac app, and connected apps for Canva, Dropbox, Instacart, OpenTable, and Zillow ([What's new for Gemini Spark](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17171264?hl=en)). On September 10, 2026, Google released a Gemini app for Windows where you can "hand off multi-step tasks to Gemini Spark" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-app-now-on-windows/)).

Google's help page sets the working limits ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215?hl=en)):

- **Account:** you must be 18 or over, have Keep Activity on, and use a personal Google Account. Spark "isn't available if you sign in with a work or school Google Account."
- **Concurrency:** up to 15 tasks running at a time; a scheduled task does not run while 15 are active.
- **Approvals:** Spark pauses for review before sending messages, changing your data, making purchases, or submitting web forms, and asks for confirmation on every task that involves web browsing.
- **Browser:** your own desktop Chrome, which needs your device awake, or a remote browser that pauses at sign-in pages until you take over.

For a wider field, our guide to [Gemini Spark alternatives](/blog/gemini-spark-alternatives/) compares seven options. This page is the head-to-head with Construct.

### Is Gemini Spark available for Google AI Pro users?

Yes in the US, not yet elsewhere. Google's changelog says it began "rolling out Gemini Spark in English to Google AI Pro subscribers in the US" on July 16, 2026, and the help page lists Pro or Ultra as the requirement. Google's US plan card still names Spark only under Ultra. Outside the US, the Google pages we reviewed document Spark for Ultra only.

What this means for your budget:

- **Starting from zero:** Construct Lite costs about $11 a month less than Google AI Pro and about $91 less than Ultra.
- **Already paying for Google AI Pro:** for $9 more, Construct adds a team workspace that works across Google and the rest of your stack, with files, schedules, and memory your team can see.
- **A team of five:** Construct Starter at $59 covers up to 5 agents for the whole workspace. Spark is a personal agent on each person's own account.

## Does Gemini Spark work with a Google Workspace business account?

No. Google's help page says Spark is not available when you sign in with a work or school Google Account, which rules out most small companies whose email, calendar, and files live in Google Workspace ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215?hl=en)). On May 19, 2026, Google said "Gemini Spark in Google Workspace will be available soon in preview for business customers in the Gemini app" ([Google Workspace blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/workspace/workspace-updates/)). A preview date for Workspace accounts is not documented in the sources we reviewed.

Construct connects to Gmail and Google Calendar through its live integration catalog, with whichever account you authorize, including a Workspace account. If your Workspace admin restricts third-party apps, Google lets them mark an app as "Trusted, Specific Google data, Limited, or Blocked," so ask them to allow the connection first ([Google Workspace Admin Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/apps/control-which-apps-access-google-workspace-data?hl=en)). Our guide to [letting an AI agent use your Gmail safely](/blog/ai-agent-gmail-access-safely/) covers the scopes to grant and what to keep as drafts.

## Is Gemini Spark available in the UK and EU?

No. Google lists Spark as available wherever Gemini Apps are supported "except in the European Economic Area, Nigeria, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215?hl=en)), and no change to that list was documented on Google's pages when we checked. Construct does not depend on a Google AI subscription, so Google's regional list does not apply to it; confirm your country at checkout.

## How to test Construct against Spark yourself

The fairest test is one recurring job, the same prompt on both, for a full working week. We have not published a measured run, so this is a checklist for your own.

1. **Pick a weekday inbox digest.** Use this prompt on both: "Every weekday at 8:00, read yesterday's and this morning's Gmail. List emails that still need a reply, oldest first, with a one-line summary each. Then list today's Calendar events with attendees. Save the digest as a dated file. Do not send or reply to anything."
2. **Use the account you really work in.** If that is a Workspace account, Spark cannot run the test; connect it to Construct from the integration catalog.
3. **Check the first three mornings by hand.** Count missed emails and false alarms, and note every confirmation prompt.
4. **Close your laptop for the rest of the week.** Calendar schedules in Construct run on its servers; see whether each digest still arrives.
5. **Ask a teammate to open Thursday's digest.** In Construct it is a file in the shared workspace.
6. **Add up the cost** for everyone who needs the digest, not just you.

On the Construct side, once the first mornings are right, save the job as a workflow and put it on the Calendar, where it uses one of Lite's 3 scheduled tasks. Our guides to [inbox triage with drafts, not sends](/blog/ai-inbox-triage/) and [running an AI agent on a schedule](/blog/ai-agent-scheduled-tasks/) walk through both halves.

## Already using Spark? Add Construct in 15 minutes

You do not have to give up Spark. Start with the job that touches a work account, a teammate, or a tool outside Google.

1. **Start on Lite for $9, or take the 7-day Pro trial** to test your own model keys and longer runs.
2. **Copy the instructions and skill** you set up in Spark into a Construct chat, and save the Docs or Sheets it uses into the workspace.
3. **Connect Gmail, Google Calendar, and the other apps** the job needs from the integration catalog.
4. **Run it once and check the result** in Files.
5. **Put it on the Calendar** so it runs every weekday whether or not anyone is online.

There is no import: Spark's skills, schedules, and remote browser data do not transfer, and Google's help page says turning Spark off deletes its remote browser and remote code execution data, so save anything you need first. Write your rules into the first instruction and Construct saves them as memory you can read. One thing to set up deliberately: Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so for replies and anything customer-facing, ask for drafts and send them yourself once you have checked them.

## When might Spark still fit?

- You run a one-person business from a personal Gmail account and already pay for Google AI Pro in the US.
- The work lives almost entirely in Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Maps, where Spark's native access goes deepest.
- You want a confirmation before every send and purchase built in, or need the agent to use your local Chrome or Mac.

For recurring work on a business account, shared with a team, and crossing Google and other tools, Construct is the better fit.

It starts at $9 instead of $19.99 to $99.99, works with the Workspace account your business actually runs on, keeps every finished file in a workspace your team shares, and reaches the business tools outside Google.
