## Why a team works better than one AI agent

One agent asked to research, write, check facts and report numbers does all four badly. Keep each agent's job narrow, because mixing two or more roles makes quality drop fast. A team also makes problems easy to trace: when a draft sounds wrong, you fix the writer's instructions, not everything at once.

In Construct each agent has a role, a lead agent it reports to, and only the apps its job needs. Their output lands in shared workspace files, so the writer can read the researcher's report and you can open both.

## Step 1: set up the lead agent and your brand files

The lead is your head of content. It runs the other agents, keeps the brand files current, and sends you one brief a day. In Construct, the built-in Construct agent takes this role.

Connect the apps you use for content from the integration catalog, such as X, Notion, Google Drive and Gmail. Then ask the lead to build three files in the workspace:

- `voice.md`: how you write, pulled from your last 50 posts, with phrases you use and phrases you never use.
- `audience.md`: who you are writing for, what they already know, and what they want from you.
- `pillars.md`: the three to five topics you post about, with an example post for each.

Prompt for the lead:

<Prompt>

Read my last 50 posts on X and my Notion content database. Write voice.md, audience.md and pillars.md in the workspace. Then interview me with ten questions about anything you could not work out, and update the files with my answers.

</Prompt>

Every other agent reads these files before it works, and you correct them as you go. Construct's memory also keeps what you tell an agent between runs, and you can open it, fix a fact or delete it.

### Researcher

<Prompt>

Every weekday at 6am, scan the accounts in niche-accounts.md and the topics in pillars.md. Find 15 posts doing at least three times better than that account's usual. For each, note the hook, the format and why it worked, and turn it into one idea that fits my pillars. Save it as research/DATE.md.

</Prompt>

### Writer

<Prompt>

Read voice.md and today's research file. Pick the five strongest ideas and write three hook options and one full draft for each. Match my voice exactly and never use the phrases listed under "never" in voice.md. Save drafts/DATE.md. Do not post anything.

</Prompt>

### Fact checker

<Prompt>

Read today's drafts. Check every number, name and claim against a primary source in the browser. Mark each one as confirmed, wrong or unverifiable, with the source link, and save checks/DATE.md.

</Prompt>

### Analyst

<Prompt>

Pull yesterday's impressions, replies, new followers and number of posts from X. Compare them with the 7-day average and note the best and worst post. Save stats/DATE.md.

</Prompt>

### The lead's daily brief

<Prompt>

At 8am, read today's research, drafts, checks and stats. Send me one message with the three drafts most worth posting, any fact that failed the check, and yesterday's numbers. Keep it under 200 words.

</Prompt>

## Should AI agents publish for you?

No. Let the agents research, draft and check, and edit every angle, topic and draft yourself before it goes out. That is the right default. Construct's workflows do not pause for approval yet, so keep the team at the draft stage and post yourself. The agents do the hours of scanning and first drafts, and the taste stays yours.

## Keep usage and quality under control

- Run only the agents you need. Keep three or four active and give only a few a daily schedule, because running all of them burns usage.
- Ask agents to be brief. Short reports cost less and are faster to read.
- Keep one file per agent with its tasks, dos and don'ts, and point the agent at it.
- Correct the voice file every time a draft does not sound like you, and say why. The writer gets better each week.
- Let the lead bundle everything into one brief, so you read one message instead of five.
